Reviews give asobeast two things a listing cannot: the words your users actually use, and an early signal that something in the latest release is going wrong.

## Sync reviews

Reviews sync as part of the daily pipeline, which starts at `CRON_DAILY` and defaults to 03:00 UTC. Each review is stored once, keyed by the app and the store review id, so re running a sync does not duplicate rows.

A reviewer on Google Play can edit a review after it was posted, and the store keeps the same review id. The daily sync takes the newer score, title, text, version and date of an edited review it already stored, and sends no alert for it. When the store also shows a developer reply that is older than the edit, the review is **Updated after reply**, described under [Reply to reviews](#reply-to-reviews).

A Google Play review can carry a star rating and no text. It is stored like any other review, shows as "No written review" on the reviews page, counts in the star filters and the negative review totals, and sends a `review.negative` alert that reads "no written review" when its rating is at or below `ALERT_REVIEW_SCORE_MAX`. A review whose text is only white space is shown the same way. It contributes nothing to the keyword ideas mined from review text.

Review sync stays on the home market, like category ranks and the ratings history. See [Countries and markets](/concepts/countries).

Stored reviews are listed on the app's reviews page, filtered by star, version, period and reply state, sorted, searched, and exported to CSV. The period filter keeps the reviews written in the last 30, 90 or 180 days, the same windows as the ratings chart, so it answers what came in since a release. The version filter lists the newest version first and compares the numbers in each version, so `3.123.0` ranks above `3.95.0` and `450.0.0.24.77` above `6.12.2`. See [Export reviews to CSV](/guides/export-data#export-reviews-to-csv).

## See what people mention

Above the list, **What people mention** shows the phrases that stand out in two rows. **Complaints** come up mostly in reviews at or below `ALERT_REVIEW_SCORE_MAX` stars, such as `server problem` or `credit card`. **Praise** comes up mostly in the other reviews. A phrase that reviews of every rating use is neither, so it is not shown. The strip shows up to six complaints and four praise phrases, the strongest first, and is hidden when nothing stands out.

Each phrase is compared with the newest 500 reviews in the version and period filters, so the strip still names complaints when the star filter keeps only one star reviews. When most of those reviews are high rated, a complaint has to stand out clearly against them, while a phrase counts as praise as soon as it leans as positive as they do. When most are low rated, the roles swap, so a bad release still lists its complaints. The number on a phrase counts the first 200 reviews that match every filter in the list's order, the same reviews a search reaches, and a phrase needs at least two of them. Pressing a phrase searches the list for it, and pressing it again clears the search.

A phrase holds up to four words and never starts or ends with the app's own name, a word such as `app`, `good`, `love`, `use` or `time`, or a function word of the review's language, so a Spanish review in a United States storefront adds no `que`. When a longer phrase covers most mentions of a shorter one, only the longer is shown, so `encountered a fatal error` replaces `fatal error`. The phrases come from the same extraction as the keyword ideas described under [Mine reviews for keyword ideas](#mine-reviews-for-keyword-ideas). No AI is involved and no store request is made.

## Reply to reviews

Every review on the reviews page has a **Reply** button that opens a composer under it. Write the reply yourself or, with `OPENAI_API_KEY` set, press **Draft with AI**. Edit the text, press **Copy**, open the store console with the link beside it, paste the reply there and press **Mark as replied**. asobeast never writes to the store, so the reply is posted only when you paste it. What a draft sends and what it costs is on [Enable the optional AI features](/guides/ai-features).

The text is saved on the review as you leave the field and whenever you press a button, so a reload or a teammate sees the same draft and a draft is never paid for twice. **Discard** removes the text and the replied mark. The counter under the text measures it against the store's limit, and the buttons wait until the text fits. The limits and the two sources of a replied review are explained once, under [Developer replies](/concepts/stores#developer-replies).

Each review shows one of four states. **Needs reply** has no reply anywhere, **Draft saved** has a reply kept here that is not marked, and **Replied** is either a reply Google Play already shows or a review you marked. The badge says which: **Replied on Google Play** or **Marked replied**. **Updated after reply** is a Google Play review the reviewer edited after the developer reply the store shows, often with a new rating, so it is worth reading again. That state appears in the counts and the filter only while a review is in it. The counts at the top of the list filter it by state, and pressing **Needs reply** also sorts the lowest scores first, because a reply queue is worked worst first. The toolbar sorts the newest or the lowest scores first and searches the titles, texts, authors and replies of the reviews loaded on the page. The page loads 50 reviews, and **Show all** or a search raises that to 200.

### Translate a review

With AI configured, a review written in a language other than your browser's shows **Translate** next to its author. Pressing it spends one AI call and shows the title and text in your language with the language it was translated from. **Show original** and **Show translation** switch back and forth without another call, because the translation is kept on the review, so a reload or a teammate who reads the same language sees it for free. Translating into another language replaces the kept translation, and a reviewer's edit clears it. A translation that adds a link or an address the review does not contain is refused, and the call stays counted. asobeast guesses the language of a review from its words and script, and falls back to the storefront's language when a review is too short to tell. What a translation sends is on [Enable the optional AI features](/guides/ai-features).

### Reply guidelines

With AI configured, the **Reply guidelines** button on the toolbar keeps up to 1,000 characters for the app: who signs, the support address, what never to promise. Every draft for the app follows them.

### What a draft does

A draft is written in the review's language, addresses the reviewer by name when there is one and stays on the one thing the review says. It never asks for a better rating, never promotes, and names a link or an address only when your guidelines or your instruction contain it; asobeast checks the draft for that and refuses one that adds any other, which still counts as a call. The text of the review is handed to the model as data, so a review that tries to give the model instructions does not steer the draft. It suggests updating only when the review was left on an older version than the listing shows, and describes a fix only when the release notes say so. Pick a tone, friendly, neutral or formal, and add a one line instruction for this review if you want to steer it. A draft that would run past the store's limit is shortened on a sentence boundary and the composer says so.

The Action Center's unanswered reviews action opens this page filtered to the reviews that need a reply, lowest first. See [Work the Action Center](/guides/action-center).

## Read the ratings history

The ratings chart tracks the average rating and the rating count over time, taken from each snapshot rather than recomputed from individual reviews. It is the fastest way to see whether a release damaged your rating, and it feeds the ratings factor of the audit. See [Run an ASO audit](/guides/aso-audit).

## Mine reviews for keyword ideas

asobeast extracts repeated phrases from review text and offers them as keyword suggestions, alongside store suggest and competitor derived ideas. Adding one tracks it like any other keyword.

A phrase is one to three words from a single sentence of the title or of the text, never both. It never starts or ends with a stopword, a function word of the storefront's languages, which skips [a few more words in reviews than in listings](/concepts/stores#how-are-auto-tracked-keywords-chosen), or a filler such as `just`, `please`, `now` or `also`, so `crashes on launch` is offered and `to use` or `the driver` is not. A contraction reads as the word it contracts, so `don't` and `it's` add nothing on their own, and a version number such as `0.78.8` separates words rather than becoming one. The same phrases feed the review theme of the ASO audit and the review theme actions.

These ideas are worth a look because they are phrased the way users search rather than the way you describe your own product.

## Watch for a growing theme

A phrase becomes an action item when it appears in at least 3 negative reviews of the latest version and at least twice as often as it did in the previous version's reviews. The rule needs at least 5 reviews on the version before it will fire, so a single angry review is never enough.

The action resolves when the version is superseded without the theme recurring, or when mentions fall below the minimum. At most 3 review themes open per app per run. See [Work the Action Center](/guides/action-center).

## Alert on negative reviews

`ALERT_REVIEW_SCORE_MAX` sets the highest star rating treated as negative. It defaults to `2` and accepts 1 to 4, so the default fires on one and two star reviews.

Subscribe a webhook or an email channel to `review.negative` to receive them. See [Send alerts](/guides/alerts).

The review backfill queued at import, described in [Import an app](/guides/import-an-app), stores its reviews without sending alerts, so an import does not alert on reviews that were already on the store. Every later sync alerts for each new review at or below `ALERT_REVIEW_SCORE_MAX`, so when the backfill comes back empty, the first daily sync that finds reviews alerts on them.

The dashboard's new low ratings and the weekly digest's negative review count answer a different question. They count reviews at or below `ALERT_REVIEW_SCORE_MAX` that were written in the window, using the date the store gives each review, so reviews stored by the backfill count only when they were written that week. A review with no store date counts from the day asobeast stored it.

## Troubleshooting

- **No reviews appear.** The store returns reviews for the home storefront, and a new or low volume app may genuinely have none in the window the store exposes.
- **The rating count moves without new reviews.** Ratings and reviews are different things. Users can rate without writing.
- **A theme did not fire.** Check that the version has at least 5 reviews and that the phrase reached 3 negative mentions with twice the previous version's rate.
- **Translate is missing.** No `OPENAI_API_KEY` is set, or the review is already in your browser's language. A kept translation still shows without a key.
- **A review moved to Updated after reply.** The reviewer edited it on Google Play after the developer reply. Read it again and update the reply in Play Console if needed.
- **Draft with AI is missing.** No `OPENAI_API_KEY` is set. With the monthly AI allowance spent the button stays but is disabled until the 1st.
- **The badge says replied but I never replied here.** Google Play shows a reply someone posted in Play Console, and asobeast reads it with the next daily sync.
- **Drafting fails with "added a link or an address".** The model put a link or an email address in the draft that your guidelines do not contain. Draft again, or add the address to the guidelines if it is yours.
- **My reply is cut.** It ran past the store's limit. Shorten it or edit it before you copy it.
- **A review has stars and no text.** Google Play lets users rate without writing, and a few old reviews on the store carry only the rating. They are kept so the star counts stay right.
