A competitor is a listing asobeast tracks alongside yours so you can compare metadata and see where it ranks on the keywords you care about.

## Add a competitor

Two paths, both on the competitors tab of an app.

| Path | When to use it |
| --- | --- |
| From a store URL | You already know which app you want to watch |
| From a stored SERP snapshot | You want the apps that actually outrank you on a keyword |

The second path is usually better, because it names the apps the store already puts in front of your users rather than the ones you assume are competitors.

One listing in one storefront is either an app you track or a competitor of one app, never both and never two at once. Adding a listing you already track as an app, importing one you already watch as a competitor, or attaching a competitor that already belongs to another app all answer `409` and change nothing. Remove the listing from where it is first.

Adding a competitor this app already tracks returns that competitor without reaching the store and without writing a snapshot, for the same reason a repeat import does. See [Importing twice costs nothing extra](/guides/import-an-app#importing-twice-costs-nothing-extra).

## Why are competitors free to track?

A position check searches the keyword's storefront once and records positions for the primary app and for every competitor attached to it from that single search.

So adding a competitor costs zero extra store requests on keywords you already track. Ten competitors on a keyword are still one search per day. Adding a _keyword_, or a market, is what costs budget. See [Countries and markets](/concepts/countries).

## Compare listings

The comparison view puts your listing beside each competitor field by field, with character counts against the store limits.

The gap analysis is the useful half: it shows phrases competitors index that you do not, so you can decide whether they belong in your own title, subtitle, keyword field or short description. Adding one from there tracks it with the source `COMPETITOR`, which starts its relevance lower than a keyword drawn from your own listing. See [Popularity, difficulty and opportunity](/concepts/scoring).

## Read the comparison matrix

The matrix lists every tracked keyword with your position and each competitor's. The first column shows the flag of the market the keyword is tracked in, so the same phrase tracked in two markets is two rows you can tell apart. Click any header to sort by it, your own column and each competitor included, and click again to reverse. Search narrows the keywords by text, the market filter keeps the rows of one market and appears once you track keywords in more than one, and the result filter keeps the rows where you are **winning** (you rank best, or alone), **losing** (a competitor ranks better, or ranks where you do not) or **tied**. The only gaps switch still asks the server for gap rows alone.

Each position is shaded by its grade and the best position in a row stays underlined. Popularity and difficulty carry their grade too. See [How are scores graded?](/concepts/scoring#how-are-scores-graded).

Click a keyword to open a panel that lists you and every competitor with the position each holds and what its listing says for that keyword. The panel reads the title and the subtitle, or the short description on Google Play, of the listing in the keyword's market. For your own app it also reads the App Store keyword field you entered, when the keyword is tracked in your home market. An App Store market searches every language version of a listing it offers, so the panel also reads those for each app where asobeast has captured them, shows the ones that match under the name of their language, and names the ones it could not check. Words in two different language versions do not combine into one phrase, so each version is counted on its own.

Each competitor that ranks above you carries a one line reason, picked by fixed rules from what the panel can see: a stronger place for the keyword (title before subtitle before keyword field, and an intact phrase before scattered words), a much larger number of ratings in that market, no visible match at all, or language versions that could not be checked. Your own card says how many competitors rank above you, which words no single listing of yours covers, and whether your listing text changed in the last four weeks, because ranks usually take two to four weeks to settle. These are rules of thumb. Neither store publishes how it weighs a field, and installs, the strongest signal, are not visible for other apps. It marks the keyword's words where they appear and says whether a field holds the exact phrase, some of the words or none. It shows the rating beside them. An app can still rank without the words showing, because search also reads fields only the developer sees, such as the App Store keyword field.

**Export CSV** in the matrix writes the rows shown, in the order shown, and the competitor list above it exports as well. See [Export the keyword comparison to CSV](/guides/export-data#export-the-keyword-comparison-to-csv).

Discovery sorts the same way, by appearances, keywords, best position, average position or rating, and searches by app name.

## Watch competitor metadata changes

Competitor listings are snapshotted on the same schedule as your own, and a field level change appears in the changes feed marked as a competitor change. Alerts can carry them in a separate competitor report. See [Watch listing changes](/guides/listing-changes).

## Troubleshooting

- **A competitor shows no positions.** It only gets positions on keywords you track, in the storefronts you track them in.
- **A competitor's ratings column is empty on Google Play.** Google Play search results carry no rating counts.
- **You added the wrong app.** Removing a competitor removes its position history with it, so confirm the store id before adding.
