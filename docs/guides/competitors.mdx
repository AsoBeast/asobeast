A competitor is a listing asobeast tracks alongside yours so you can compare metadata and see where it ranks on the keywords you care about.

## Add a competitor

Four paths, the first three on the competitors tab of an app.

| Path | When to use it |
| --- | --- |
| By name | You know the app but not its URL. The form searches your app's store and storefront and lists the results with their icon |
| From a store URL | You already know which app you want to watch |
| From a stored SERP snapshot | You want the apps that actually outrank you on a keyword |
| From the suggestions in the guided setup | Right after an import, the apps that rank in your keyword results and the apps the store calls similar are listed with a checkbox each and added together |

The second path is usually better, because it names the apps the store already puts in front of your users rather than the ones you assume are competitors.

One listing in one storefront is either an app you track or a competitor of one app, never both and never two at once. Adding a listing you already track as an app, importing one you already watch as a competitor, or attaching a competitor that already belongs to another app all answer `409` and change nothing. Remove the listing from where it is first.

Adding a competitor this app already tracks returns that competitor without reaching the store and without writing a snapshot, for the same reason a repeat import does. See [Importing twice costs nothing extra](/guides/import-an-app#importing-twice-costs-nothing-extra).

## Suggested competitors

The competitor step of the guided setup suggests up to ten apps from two sources, in this order.

| Source | What it is | Shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keyword results | Untracked apps that appear in your own keyword results over the last 30 days | Their icon, how often they appeared and their best position |
| Similar apps | The apps the store lists as similar to yours | Their icon |

A keyword result counts every storefront you track keywords in, so an app that ranks in your German results is suggested with those appearances. Its title, developer, rating and icon are the ones your home storefront shows when the app ranked there during the window, and the newest result of another storefront when it did not. An app is therefore named the same way every day, in the language of your own listing.

Google Play lists popular apps from unrelated categories among the similar apps, so a Google Play suggestion is kept only when its title and description share at least half as many of the words and phrases in your title and short description as the closest similar app does, and the ones sharing the most come first. The apps you already track and the keyword results are left out before the closest one is chosen. The App Store list is kept in the store's order.

Your own app and the competitors you already track are never suggested, and an app both sources name is listed once, as a keyword result. Reading the keyword results costs nothing, because they come from the searches the daily run already made. The similar apps lookup reaches the store while you wait and spends one store lookup of the hourly allowance. When the store does not answer or the hourly allowance is spent, the keyword result apps are still listed and the similar apps are left out. `GET /apps/{id}/competitors/suggestions` answers the same list, and so does the `competitor_suggestions` MCP tool.

## Why are competitors free to track?

A position check searches the keyword's storefront once and records positions for the primary app and for every competitor attached to it from that single search.

So adding a competitor costs zero extra store requests on keywords you already track. Ten competitors on a keyword are still one search per day. Adding a _keyword_, or a market, is what costs budget. See [Countries and markets](/concepts/countries).

## Compare listings

The comparison view puts your listing beside each competitor field by field, with character counts against the store limits.

The gap analysis is the useful half: it shows phrases competitors index that you do not, so you can decide whether they belong in your own title, subtitle, keyword field or short description. Adding one from there tracks it with the source `COMPETITOR`, which starts its relevance lower than a keyword drawn from your own listing. See [Popularity, difficulty and opportunity](/concepts/scoring). For where competitors outrank you on the keywords you already track, see [Gap analysis](#gap-analysis).

## Collapse a section

The competitors tab has four sections: the add form together with the competitors you track, **Gap analysis**, **Apps you don’t track yet**, and the comparison matrix. Each section collapses behind its title. The window tabs of the discovery section and the only gaps switch of the matrix stay beside their titles while the section is collapsed. The choice is remembered in this browser and is not part of the link. When you print the page, a collapsed section prints its title and nothing else.

## Read the comparison matrix

The matrix lists every tracked keyword with your position and each competitor's. Click any header to sort by it, your own column and each competitor included, and click again to reverse. Search narrows the keywords by text, and the result filter keeps the rows where you are **winning** (you rank best, or alone), **losing** (a competitor ranks better, or ranks where you do not) or **tied**. The only gaps switch still asks the server for gap rows alone.

When you track keywords in more than one market, the first column names the market of each row, so the same phrase tracked in two storefronts is two rows you can tell apart. On a phone the column is hidden to leave room for the positions, and the market appears under the keyword instead. **Columns** shows or hides the column on any screen, and the choice is remembered in this browser. The **Market** filter beside **Result** keeps the rows of one market, is part of the link, and clears with **Clear all**. See [Countries and markets](/concepts/countries).

Each position is shaded by its grade and the best position in a row stays underlined. Popularity and difficulty carry their grade too. See [How are scores graded?](/concepts/scoring#how-are-scores-graded).

A dash in place of a position means no check has run yet for that app and keyword, which is how a competitor you just added looks until the next keyword check. `>200` means the check ran and did not find the app within the captured depth.

**Export CSV** in the matrix writes the rows shown, in the order shown, and the competitor list above it exports as well. See [Export the keyword comparison to CSV](/guides/export-data#export-the-keyword-comparison-to-csv).

Discovery sorts the same way, by appearances, keywords, best position, average position or rating, and searches by app name.

To find searches around what competitors target, use **Competitor searches** in **Suggestions** on the keywords page. See [Get keyword suggestions](/guides/track-keywords#get-keyword-suggestions).

## Gap analysis

The **Gap analysis** section of the competitors tab sorts the keywords you track into four counted lists. It reads the same daily checks as the matrix, so it costs no extra store request.

| List | A keyword is in it when |
| --- | --- |
| **Keyword gaps** | A competitor ranks in the top 30 and you are unranked or below 100 |
| **Winnable now** | It is a keyword gap and its difficulty is below its popularity, or the best competitor ranks 11 or lower |
| **Threats** | You rank in the top 30 and a competitor is **closing in**, 1 to 3 places below you and higher than on its previous check, or **overtook you**, passing you in the last 7 days and still ahead |
| **You lead** | You rank and you are ahead of every competitor that ranks, or none of them ranks |

Each list is sorted by opportunity, then by popularity. A row names the market, the best competitor with both positions, the popularity, the difficulty and the opportunity. A threat also says why, such as `Closing in, rose 2` or `Overtook you on Nov 1`, and a lead says by how many places.

### Which competitors count

A delisted competitor never counts, in any list. A competitor you added after the last daily check is not compared until its first check, so no list claims you lead over an app nobody looked at. A keyword whose latest check is older than yesterday UTC stays in **Keyword gaps** with a clock that names the day of the last check, and is left out of **Threats**, because a threat needs a fresh check of both apps.

### Reading an empty list

An empty list says why it is empty:

- **Add a competitor to see gaps** when you track none.
- **Every competitor you track is delisted** when none of them can be compared.
- **Your competitors have not been checked yet** when no keyword of the market was compared, for example right after you add a competitor or a market.
- **No keyword gaps right now** only when the comparison ran and found nothing.

### Filter, search and export

The **market** select appears when your keywords span markets and keeps the rows of one. The tab, the market, the search and the sort are part of the link. **Export CSV** writes the rows of the open tab, in the order shown. See [Export the gap lists to CSV](/guides/export-data#export-the-gap-lists-to-csv). Pressing a keyword opens its breakdown, described in [Why does a competitor rank above you?](#why-does-a-competitor-rank-above-you).

### The Gap badge in the matrix is stricter on purpose

The **Gap** badge of the matrix marks a keyword where a competitor ranks in the top 10 and you rank below 30. The **Keyword gaps** list asks a wider question, a competitor in the top 30 and you below 100. Both stay as they are. The `theyRankYouDont`, `youRankTheyDont` and `outranked` lists of `GET /apps/{id}/competitors/analysis` are unchanged too; the four lists answer beside them with a `coverage` block.

### The overview line

Under the visibility chart of the overview, one sentence sums up the market you are looking at, for example `2 winnable gaps. Biggest: productivity app, Rival Focus ranks #8, you are not ranked. 2 threats.` with a link to the biggest gap. It reads `No winnable gaps or threats right now.` when the comparison found nothing, and one of the sentences above when nothing could be compared.

## Why does a competitor rank above you?

Press a keyword in the matrix to open its breakdown. Your app comes first, then every competitor, each with its position on the latest daily check.

For each app the panel reads every listing the keyword's market searches. On the App Store that is the title and subtitle of each localization the storefront reads, plus the keyword fields you saved for those localizations. A competitor's keyword field is private and is never shown. On Google Play it is the title, the short description and the long description, and the text of the long description is not shown. See [App Store and Google Play](/concepts/stores) and [Which localizations does a storefront read?](/concepts/countries#which-localizations-does-a-storefront-read).

The keyword's words are marked where they appear. Each field says whether it holds the whole phrase, every word or some of them, which is the same verdict the coverage table uses. See [Check keyword coverage](/guides/metadata-workbench#check-keyword-coverage). Words of two localizations never combine into one phrase, so each localization is judged on its own. See [What does each store index?](/concepts/stores#what-does-each-store-index).

A word that matches only when accents are ignored, such as `guidees` for `guidées`, has a dotted mark and does not count as a match. Stores usually treat the two spellings alike, but neither store documents it.

**Not read** names the localizations the market searches whose listing has not been captured for that app. For a competitor only the native localizations of an English default storefront are captured, so the others stay unread rather than guessed. When the keyword's market has no stored listing of an app, the panel reads its home listing and says so.

A competitor that ranks above you carries one note, from the first rule that applies:

| Note | When it applies | What it suggests |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Placement | Its strongest placement beats yours. The order is the title, then the subtitle or short description, then your keyword field, then the long description, then every word spread over fields, then some of the words | Move the keyword to a stronger field |
| Spelling | It spells a word exactly as searched where your listings match only with accents ignored | Consider the exact spelling |
| Ratings | Both ratings come from the keyword's market, and it has at least three times your ratings and at least 100 more | Ratings may be the difference |
| Not read | No listing it was read in holds every word of the keyword, and a localization the market searches was not captured | The missing listing may hold the keyword |
| Words not all visible | App Store only, and no listing it was read in holds every word of the keyword | It may rank through its private keyword field |
| Similar | None of the above | Nothing visible explains the gap |

These are rules of thumb. Installs and engagement, the strongest signals, are not visible for other apps, and neither store publishes how it weighs a field.

For **Not read** and **Words not all visible** the card says whether none of the words show or only some of them, the same count the field badges give.

Your own card says how many competitors rank above you, which words none of your listings holds, which match only with accents ignored, and whether your listing changed or you saved a keyword field this market reads in the last 28 days, because ranks usually take two to four weeks to settle. The panel reads stored data only and makes no store request.

## Watch competitor metadata changes

Competitor listings are snapshotted on the same schedule as your own, and a field level change appears in the changes feed marked as a competitor change. Alerts can carry them in a separate competitor report. See [Watch listing changes](/guides/listing-changes).

## Troubleshooting

- **A competitor shows no positions.** It only gets positions on keywords you track, in the storefronts you track them in. A dash means its first check has not run yet; it fills in on the next keyword check.
- **A competitor's ratings column is empty on Google Play.** Google Play search results carry no rating counts.
- **You added the wrong app.** Removing a competitor removes its position history with it, so confirm the store id before adding.
