Keyword discovery finds phrases your app ranks for, or could rank for, that you do not track yet. A weekly job and a manual refresh build the list, check where the app stands on each phrase, and keep it for you to review. Nothing is tracked until you say so, unless you switch on auto track. Building the list reads no store at all; checking it spends a capped number of searches.

## Where candidates come from

| Source | What it reads | Markets |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Your listing | The title and subtitle of the listing in each market (the title and short description on Google Play), in every localization the storefront captures | Every market of the app |
| Competitors | The titles and subtitles of your competitors' home listings | The home market only |
| Reviews | Phrases of two words or more that at least three of your newest reviews use | The home market only |
| Apple popularity | Popular terms of the app's genre in the newest week Apple lists, that share a word with your listing | App Store only, and only when the instance has [Apple Ads configured](/configuration/reference) |
| Your other apps | Phrases the workspace tracks for its other apps in the same store and market | Every market of the app |

Competitors and reviews feed the home market only, because asobeast captures both for the home storefront.

One phrase found by several sources is listed once, under the source with the highest starting relevance. A phrase the app tracks in that market, active or paused, is left out, and so is a phrase you dismissed. Each market keeps the best 200 candidates, ordered by relevance and then popularity.

## How the rank is found

When another search of the same phrase in the same storefront ran today or yesterday, for any workspace on the instance, its stored results answer for free. They hold the top 50, so a discovery read this way shows depth 50. A phrase whose stored results do not contain your app may still rank between 51 and 200, so it stays due for a search of its own. Otherwise one search reads the top 200 for the phrase.

The rank column reads like the tracked table: a number is the position, `>50` or `>200` means checked and not found within that depth, and a dash means not checked yet.

A search of a phrase nobody tracks stores its results for everybody, exactly as a daily check does. A search of a phrase some workspace tracks stores nothing, because the daily run owns that day's results. A discovery never writes a position history for your app until you track it.

## The weekly cap

Each app gets at most 40 searches a week on the App Store and 20 on Google Play, where one search costs about eight requests. The week starts on Monday at 00:00 UTC. **Refresh** shares the same cap, so a refresh after the Monday run usually finds nothing left to search, though it still rebuilds the list and reads other searches for free. The cap applies on self hosted instances too, because it protects the address of the machine that runs asobeast.

The weekly run starts on `CRON_DISCOVERY`, Monday 07:30 UTC by default; an empty value switches it off. See the [configuration reference](/configuration/reference). Its searches queue behind the daily run and never delay it. A phrase is searched again after seven days.

Refresh spends one deep search of the plan's on demand allowance, and only when it queues at least one search. See [Rate limits and quotas](/api-reference/rate-limits).

## States

| State | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Ranked | The app has a position for the phrase |
| Opportunities | Not ranked, relevance at least 60 and opportunity at least 35 |
| All | Every open discovery |

**Recommended** marks the first 50 in list order.

Opportunity needs popularity and difficulty, and a discovery is never scored: a score costs several store requests and would spend the weekly budget many times over. Popularity and difficulty therefore come from a score some tracker of the phrase already produced, or, for an App Store term Apple lists, from Apple's own popularity. Opportunities is often empty until keywords are scored. Tracking a discovery queues its first score.

## Track, dismiss and auto track

Tracking a discovery spends the plan's keyword room the way a manual add does. What does not fit stays a discovery, and the answer says how many did not fit your plan. A phrase another app of the workspace already tracks in that market costs no extra room. A position found today or yesterday becomes the first position of the tracked keyword; every other phrase gets its first check within minutes.

The tracked keyword's source is Suggested for review, popularity and other app phrases, and Title, Subtitle, Description or Competitor for the others. See [Popularity, difficulty and opportunity](/concepts/scoring).

Dismiss is permanent for the phrase. If you stop tracking a phrase that came from discovery, it returns to the list with what discovery last observed.

**Auto track ranked** tracks every ranked discovery that fits at once, and every later search that finds the app tracks its phrase too. Switching it off leaves tracked keywords alone.

## The Discovered tab

The keywords page has two tabs, **Tracked** and **Discovered**, each with its count. A dot on **Discovered** says there are discoveries to review. The tab lists the discoveries of the market the market chips select, and the chips are the same as on the tracked table. The tab and its sub tab are kept in the page address (`?tab=discovered&state=ranked`), so a link opens the same view.

| Sub tab | Shows |
| --- | --- |
| All | Every open discovery of the market |
| Ranked | Discoveries the app has a position for |
| Opportunities | Discoveries that are not ranked yet and are worth tracking, as [States](#states) explains |

| Column | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| Keyword | The phrase, with a **Recommended** badge on the first 50 of the list |
| Source | Your title, Your subtitle, Your description (Your short description on Google Play), Competitors, Reviews, Apple popularity or Your other apps |
| Rank | The position, `>50` or `>200` when checked and not found within that depth, a dash when not checked yet |
| Popularity, Difficulty | The scores some tracker already produced, a dash when there is none |
| Relevance | How close the phrase is to your listing |
| Opportunity | Popularity weighed against difficulty, a dash until both exist |

The list keeps the order the API answers, relevance first and then popularity, and shows 50 rows at a time with **Show 50 more** below. The line under the table says when the list was last checked, how many searches the week allows and how many were used, and the day of the next weekly run. While searches are still queued it counts them and the list updates by itself. When auto track is on and some ranked discoveries did not fit the plan, it says how many.

## Track, dismiss and refresh

Each row has **Track** and **Dismiss**. Tick rows to use **Track selected**, or use **Track recommended** for the rows with the badge and **Track all ranked** for every ranked discovery of the market; each button names how many it tracks. Nothing asks for confirmation, because a tracked keyword returns to the list when you stop tracking it. A message says how many keywords are now tracked and how many did not fit your plan.

The **Auto-track ranked** switch is the setting [Track, dismiss and auto track](#track-dismiss-and-auto-track) describes. **Refresh** rebuilds the list and queues the searches left this week. It spends one deep search of the on demand allowance only when it queues at least one search, and a refresh that finds nothing left to search says so and spends nothing.

## First results after an import

An import builds the first list from the listing it just captured and queues its searches right away, without spending an on demand allowance. The keywords step of the guided setup then shows **Your first results are ready** with the five best discoveries and their rank, and a link to the Discovered tab, or says the first results are still being collected. When the import found nothing to search, the step shows nothing, and the Discovered tab says when the next weekly run looks, or that weekly runs are off, with **Refresh** to look now. The portfolio card of the app and the tracked keywords tile of its overview name how many discoveries the app has and how many it already ranks for, for the market the overview shows.

## Through the API and MCP

| Route | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /apps/{id}/keyword-discoveries` | Lists discoveries, with `country`, `state` and `limit` |
| `POST /apps/{id}/keyword-discoveries/track` | Tracks `keywordIds`, or every ranked discovery with `allRanked: true` |
| `POST /apps/{id}/keyword-discoveries/dismiss` | Dismisses `keywordIds` |
| `POST /apps/{id}/keyword-discoveries/refresh` | Rebuilds the list and queues the searches left this week |
| `PATCH /apps/{id}/keyword-discoveries/settings` | Switches `autoTrackRanked` |

A read scoped token can list, a write scoped token can also change. Agents read the list with the `keyword_discoveries` tool; tracking is not an MCP write tool. See [asobeast API](/api-reference/introduction) and the [MCP tool reference](/mcp/tools).

## Cost and storage

`GET /jobs/budget` reports the weekly searches in a `discovery` block per store and in total, apart from the daily `total`. See [Capacity](/operations/capacity). A discovery that no run has proposed for 60 days is removed, except a dismissed one, which is kept so the phrase stays dismissed. The search results a discovery stores cost about 5 MB per app once retention settles.

## Troubleshooting

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Nothing is listed">
    The listing of the app may not be captured yet, the store may no longer list the app, or none of the sources holds a phrase. A delisted app keeps its list but gets no searches, and neither does a market where the app is not sold or where you no longer track any keyword.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The last check date never moves">
    The weekly cap may be used, the workspace may be paused (suspended, or without a plan in force on a hosted instance), or `CRON_DISCOVERY` may be empty.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
