Rankings say where your listing shows. The store funnel says whether people take it. Connect an App Store Connect key in settings and every App Store app the key covers gains a **Store funnel** card on its overview with four numbers Apple reports for it. Connect a Google Play service account and every Google Play app it can read gains the same card with three numbers Google reports. See [Google Play](#google-play).

## What you get

| Number | What it counts |
| --- | --- |
| Impressions | Times the app appeared in the App Store, on every page type |
| Product page views | Times someone opened the product page |
| Get taps | Taps on Get, the closest daily figure to a download Apple publishes in this report |
| Conversion | Get taps divided by unique impressions |

The card shows the last 28 complete days, with the change against the 28 days before and the change of the last 7 complete days against the 7 before, the same windows the [change impact](/guides/listing-changes) uses. It also says what share of the impressions came from App Store search. The card follows the market switcher like every other overview card, so **All markets** adds every storefront and a market reads that storefront alone.

An app the connection does not cover and a competitor show no numbers. A member sees the card and the connection, and only the owner connects, verifies or disconnects.

## Create the key

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the team keys">
    In App Store Connect, open **Users and Access**, then **Integrations**, then **Team Keys**. The issuer ID is shown above the list.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Generate a key with the right role">
    Use the **Admin** role the first time. Apple lets only an Admin key ask for reports. Once an Admin key has asked for them, a **Sales and Reports** key can read them from then on, and you can connect one instead.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Download the key once">
    Apple offers the `.p8` file a single time. Keep it, and note the ten character key ID beside the key.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Connect it

Open **Settings** and the **Store connections** section, press **Connect App Store Connect**, then paste the issuer ID, the key ID and the contents of the `.p8` file, or choose the file. asobeast asks Apple whether the key works before it stores anything, so a mistyped ID or a revoked key is refused on the spot.

| Control | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Verify** | Asks Apple again whether the stored key works. A key Apple refuses is marked rejected; a key it accepts again clears that and queues a sync |
| **Sync now** | Queues the download of the reports Apple published since the last sync. A sync already waiting is not queued twice |
| **Replace key** | Connects another key in place of this one |
| **Disconnect** | Deletes the stored key and stops the downloads. The numbers already stored are kept, hidden while no key reports them, and appear again once you connect a key |

When the connected key cannot ask for reports, the section says that an Admin key is needed once. Connect one, press **Sync now**, and switch back to a Sales and Reports key afterwards if you prefer.

What asobeast stores and how is on [Store console credentials](/security/store-credentials).

## How the numbers are read

asobeast asks Apple for the **App Store Discovery and Engagement** report of each app twice: once as a one time snapshot of the history Apple keeps, back to 1 January 2024, and once as an ongoing request for every new day. Apple produces the first files a day or two after the request, so the card waits for them before it shows numbers.

Every day at 05:30 UTC, after the daily run has started, asobeast downloads the files Apple published since the last sync and adds their rows to the funnel. Apple finishes a day within three days and sometimes sends a corrected file for an earlier day; the newest file always wins, and the card counts a day as complete once it is three days old and Apple has delivered it, so a sync that falls behind never shows missing days as zeros.

Apple drops rows under five users and adds a small amount of noise, so a number is close rather than exact. The card adds unique counts across markets and sources, which reads slightly high for the same person seen in two places.

## Product pages

When the key may read it, the sync also downloads the detailed version of the report, which names the custom product page behind each number. The funnel endpoint then lists each page of the last 28 complete days with its counts, the default product page first, and its conversion against the default page as a ratio. A ratio under 1 converts below the default. The [Action Center](/guides/action-center) opens `listing.cpp_underperforms` for a page with at least 100 unique impressions that converts under 0.8 of the default.

## Markets

Apple names a market by its territory. asobeast matches each territory to an App Store storefront, so a market on the switcher reads the rows of that storefront. A territory it cannot match still counts in **All markets** and is named in the API log of the sync, so the list can grow.

## Google Play

Google publishes no daily analytics API for store listings. Play Console writes monthly report files to a Cloud Storage bucket instead, and asobeast reads them through a service account you invite.

| Number | What it counts |
| --- | --- |
| Store listing visitors | People without the app who opened the store listing |
| Acquisitions | People who installed the app after opening the store listing |
| Conversion | Acquisitions divided by store listing visitors |

Google reports no impressions, so the card has three tiles.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create a service account">
    In the Google Cloud console, open **IAM and Admin**, then **Service accounts**, create an account in any project and add a key of type **JSON**. Google downloads the key file once.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Invite it in Play Console">
    In Play Console, open **Users and permissions**, invite the service account's email address and grant **View app information and download bulk reports**, for all apps or for the apps you want measured. Google can take up to a day to apply the permission.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Copy the bucket id">
    Open **Download reports** in Play Console and copy the Cloud Storage URI. The bucket id is the part after `gs://` that starts with `pubsite_prod`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect it">
    In **Settings**, **Store connections**, press **Connect Google Play**, paste the bucket id and choose the JSON key file or paste its contents. asobeast lists the bucket with the key before it stores anything, so a refused account or a wrong bucket id is answered on the spot.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Verify**, **Sync now**, **Replace service account** and **Disconnect** work as they do for App Store Connect.

### How the Play numbers are read

The daily sync lists the bucket once and, for every Google Play app of the workspace, reads two files of the current and the previous month: the country file and the traffic source file under `stats/store_performance/`. A file is downloaded only when Google wrote a new version of it since the last sync, so a sync without news downloads nothing. An app with no file in the bucket is shown as **No files in this bucket**.

A market on the switcher reads the country file. **All markets** reads the traffic source file, which splits the visitors by Google Play search, explore and third party referrers, so the card states the search share there only. The two views never add rows of both files together.

Google posts a day three to seven days after it ends, later than Apple, so the newest days of the card fill in over the following week. Google also changed how it counts store listing visitors and acquisitions on 10 July 2026, so compare periods that fall on the same side of that date.

### Installs per search term

The traffic source file also names the Google Play search terms that brought visitors. The keywords page of a Google Play app gains a **Play installs** column that shows, for each tracked keyword, the acquisitions and visitors of the newest month in which Google reported that term, with the month in its tooltip. A term matches a keyword when both read the same after asobeast normalizes them, so case, spacing and punctuation do not matter. Google reports the terms for all markets together, so the column is the same in every market, and a dash means Google reported no visitors for that term. See [Track keywords](/guides/track-keywords#see-play-installs-per-search-term).

## Limits

- One App Store Connect key and one Google Play service account per workspace.
- Apple allows each key 3,500 requests an hour. A sync downloads at most 200 daily files and leaves the rest for the next day, so a long history arrives over a few days.
- When Apple answers an error for one app, or sends a report asobeast cannot read, the other apps and reports still sync and the section says that some were left for the next sync. A request Apple stopped for inactivity is asked for again on the next sync.
- When Apple refuses the key during a sync, the connection is marked rejected and nothing is downloaded until you verify it or connect a new one. The numbers already stored are kept and the card shows them again once the key is accepted. A refused service account or a bucket that disappeared is handled the same way.
- The downloaded files are kept for `RETENTION_STORE_REPORTS_DAYS` so a parser change can read them again. The funnel rows built from them are kept.

The variables are on the [configuration reference](/configuration/reference#store-consoles).
