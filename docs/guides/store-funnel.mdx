Rankings say where your listing shows. The store funnel says whether people take it. Connect an App Store Connect key in Integrations and every App Store app the key covers gains a **Store funnel** card on its overview with four numbers Apple reports for it. The key can also import an app's private keyword fields once you turn the import on for that app, which makes them read only on the metadata page. See [When App Store Connect imports the field](/guides/metadata-workbench#when-app-store-connect-imports-the-field). Connect the Google Play Console and every Google Play app it can read gains the same card with three numbers Google reports. See [Google Play](#google-play), and [Connect a store console](#connect-a-store-console) for the setup.

## What you get

| Number | What it counts |
| --- | --- |
| Impressions | Times the app appeared in the App Store, on every page type |
| Product page views | Times someone opened the product page |
| Get taps | Taps on Get, the closest daily figure to a download Apple publishes in this report |
| Conversion | Get taps divided by unique impressions |

The card shows the last 28 complete days, with the change against the 28 days before and the change of the last 7 complete days against the 7 before, the same windows the [change impact](/guides/listing-changes) uses. Each figure carries a small line of its last 28 complete days. The card also says what share of the impressions came from App Store search. The card follows the market switcher like every other overview card, so **All markets** adds every storefront and a market reads that storefront alone.

An app the connection does not cover and a competitor show no numbers. A member sees the card and the connection, and only the owner connects, verifies or disconnects.

## How the numbers are read

asobeast asks Apple for the **App Store Discovery and Engagement** report of each app twice: once as a one time snapshot of the history Apple keeps, back to 1 January 2024, and once as an ongoing request for every new day. Apple produces the first files a day or two after the request, so the card waits for them before it shows numbers.

Every day at 05:30 UTC, after the daily run has started, asobeast downloads the files Apple published since the last sync and adds their rows to the funnel. Apple finishes a day within three days and sometimes sends a corrected file for an earlier day; the newest file always wins, and the card counts a day as complete once it is three days old and Apple has delivered it, so a sync that falls behind never shows missing days as zeros.

Apple drops rows under five users and adds a small amount of noise, so a number is close rather than exact. The card adds unique counts across markets and sources, which reads slightly high for the same person seen in two places.

## Product pages

When the key may read it, the sync also downloads the detailed version of the report, which names the custom product page behind each page view and Get tap. Apple files every impression under no page, so a page has no impressions of its own and its conversion is its Get taps divided by its product page views. The funnel endpoint then lists each page of the last 28 complete days with its counts, the default product page first and the others by page views, and its conversion against the default page as a ratio. A ratio under 1 converts below the default. The [Action Center](/guides/action-center) opens `listing.cpp_underperforms` for a page with at least 100 product page views that converts under 0.8 of the default.

## Markets

Apple names a market by its territory. asobeast matches each territory to an App Store storefront, so a market on the switcher reads the rows of that storefront. A territory it cannot match still counts in **All markets** and is named in the API log of the sync, so the list can grow.

## Google Play

Google publishes no daily analytics API for store listings. Play Console writes monthly report files to a Cloud Storage bucket instead, and asobeast reads them either with your own Google account or with a service account you invite, as [Connect Google Play](/integrations/google-play) describes.

| Number | What it counts |
| --- | --- |
| Store listing visitors | People without the app who opened the store listing |
| Acquisitions | People who installed the app after opening the store listing |
| Conversion | Acquisitions divided by store listing visitors |

Google reports no impressions, so the card has three tiles.

### How the Play numbers are read

The daily sync lists the files of every Google Play app of the workspace in the bucket and reads two files of the current and the previous month: the country file and the traffic source file under `stats/store_performance/`. A file is downloaded only when Google wrote a new version of it since the last sync, so a sync without news downloads nothing. A file over 20 MB is skipped and named in the section, since a real monthly file is a few kilobytes. An app with no file in the bucket is shown as **No files in this bucket**, and one whose files Google refuses to the service account as **No access to this app**. A file that cannot be read is tried again a few minutes later without holding back the others, and the section says so when it still cannot be read.

A market on the switcher reads the country file. **All markets** reads the traffic source file, which splits the visitors by Google Play search, explore and third party referrers, so the card states the search share there only. The two views never add rows of both files together.

Google posts a day three to seven days after it ends, later than Apple, so the newest days of the card fill in over the following week. Google also changed how it counts store listing visitors and acquisitions on 10 July 2026, so compare periods that fall on the same side of that date.

### Installs per search term

The traffic source file also names the Google Play search terms that brought visitors. Rows of Google Play search and Google Play explore count, and the placeholder Google writes when it names no term is left out. The keywords page of a Google Play app gains a **Play installs** column that shows, for each tracked keyword, the acquisitions and visitors of the newest complete month in which Google reported that term (the month in progress only when it is the only one), with the month in its tooltip. A term matches a keyword when both read the same after asobeast normalizes them, so case, spacing and punctuation do not matter. Google reports the terms for all markets together, so the column is the same in every market, and a dash means Google reported no visitors for that term. When the newest month Google has delivered names no term on any source, the column says so and names that month instead. See [Track keywords](/guides/track-keywords#see-play-installs-per-search-term).

## Limits

- One App Store Connect key and one Google Play connection, a service account key or a Google sign in, per workspace.
- Apple allows each key 3,500 requests an hour. A sync downloads at most 200 daily files on each try and leaves the rest for the next day, so a long history arrives over a few days.
- When Apple answers an error for one app, or sends a report asobeast cannot read, the other apps and reports still sync and the sync tries the ones that failed again a few minutes later. The section says that some were left for the next sync only when they still fail on the last try, so a passing error at Apple shows no warning. A request Apple stopped for inactivity, or one Apple no longer has, for example because another analytics tool on the team deleted it, is asked for again on the next sync.
- When Apple refuses the key during a sync, the connection is marked rejected and nothing is downloaded until you verify it or connect a new one. The numbers already stored are kept and the card shows them again once the key is accepted. A refused service account, a revoked Google sign in or a bucket that disappeared is handled the same way.
- The downloaded files are kept for `RETENTION_STORE_REPORTS_DAYS` so a parser change can read them again. The funnel rows built from them are kept.

The variables are on the [configuration reference](/configuration/reference#store-consoles).
