The workbench is where you see your listing the way search sees it: field by field, against the limits, and against the keywords you track.

## Read the work left

The page opens with one line that counts the work left: fields with issues, keywords the listing covers and does not cover, screenshots with a caption on the App Store, and the keyword field characters used. **keywords not covered** in that line opens the coverage table filtered to them. **Coverage by field**, beside the listing fields, shows how many tracked keywords each field carries, and its **Show uncovered** does the same.

## Lint your metadata

Each field is checked against the limit for its store, and the lint reports characters used, characters left and anything wasteful such as a duplicated word across indexed fields.

| Field | App Store | Google Play |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Title | 30 | 30 |
| Subtitle | 30 | Not present |
| Keyword field | 100 | Not present |
| Short description | Not present | 80 |
| Description | 4000, not indexed | 4000, indexed |
| Promotional text | 170, not indexed | Not present |

Fields a store does not have are hidden rather than shown empty. See [App Store and Google Play](/concepts/stores).

On Google Play, a short description word that is also in the title is a warning, not an error. Google's metadata policy asks for succinct text without repeated keywords, but it does not forbid reusing a title word, so the lint points out the wasted room without calling it a rule break. On the App Store a subtitle or keyword field word that Apple already indexes from the title stays an error, and only errors open an Action Center item.

A keyword field word is flagged as a competitor brand only when it reads as one competitor's brand. A word in your own title or subtitle, a keyword you track, a generic word such as `free`, and a word two or more competitor titles share are category vocabulary, so a geo guessing game keeps `guess` and `location` while an untracked `geoguessr` is still flagged.

## See which localizations your app ships

On an App Store app, the **App Store localizations** section lists the localizations your app ships, your home storefront's default first. Each one is an item that holds its own keyword field and shows the markets that read it as chips, **Default** or **Additional**. When the app has two markets or two shipped localizations, a matrix above the items crosses them, so you see at a glance which market reads which localization.

Each shipped localization is another title, subtitle and keyword field for search in every storefront that reads it, and the section counts that room for you. The rules, their source and their limits are in [Which localizations does a storefront read?](/concepts/countries#which-localizations-does-a-storefront-read) and [Which localizations does an app ship?](/concepts/countries#which-localizations-does-an-app-ship) The **Listing market** control above the fields shows what each of those storefronts actually serves.

These controls manage the list.

- **Add localization** adds a language you ship in App Store Connect. The dialog lists the languages your markets read first, then every other language, and searches both names and codes.
- **Find localizations** asks the store which of those languages your listing really serves and adds the ones it proves, marked **Detected**. It spends one refresh from your on demand allowance and the answers arrive as the queue drains, so the list fills in over a few minutes. It cannot see a localization whose text equals the default listing or your home listing; add that one by hand. See [Which localizations does an app ship?](/concepts/countries#which-localizations-does-an-app-ship)
- **Not listed yet**, under the items, names every localization one of your markets reads that is not on the list, with the markets that read it and an **Add** button that adds it and opens its keyword field. A localization the store serves as a market's own language arrives on its own after the next capture of that market, so this list is for a language you ship with the same text as your home listing, or for an additional language such as Spanish (Mexico) in the United States.
- **Remove localization**, at the foot of an item, deletes the localization's field and stops tracking its phrases as keyword field phrases. Phrases you track for another reason stay tracked. The home localization has no remove button.

## Check keyword coverage

Coverage answers one question per tracked keyword: does any indexed field of this listing contain it?

A field contains a keyword when it has the keyword as whole words. Chinese, Japanese, Thai, Lao, Khmer and Burmese do not put spaces between words, so a keyword in one of those scripts counts wherever it appears in the field, and a character of those scripts ends a word written next to it as a space would. The subtitle `AIが3行要約` therefore contains `ai` and `iPhone用カメラ` contains `iphone`, while `SmartNewsアプリ` does not contain `news`. Korean does put spaces between words but attaches particles to their end, so a Korean keyword counts as a whole word or as a word followed by a particle: `운동` is found in `운동을`, but not in `운동화`, and `계부` is not found in `가계부`. A keyword that mixes scripts, such as `line 漫画`, counts when each of its words is found by its own rule.

| Store | Fields that count toward coverage |
| --- | --- |
| App Store | Title, subtitle, keyword field |
| Google Play | Title, short description, description |

On the App Store a keyword also counts as covered when no field holds the whole phrase but each of its words is in the title, the subtitle or the keyword field of one localization, because App Store search matches the words of a search across those fields in any order. The coverage table marks such a keyword **Words split**: it is searchable already, and the whole phrase in the title or subtitle may still rank higher. Words from two localizations never add up, and a word that matches only with its accents ignored does not count. Google Play keeps judging the whole phrase. The overview's **Not in your metadata** list and the audit fixes that propose your best uncovered keyword follow the same verdict, so a keyword this table counts as covered never appears in either.

A keyword with high opportunity and no coverage has its own Action Center rule. See [Work the Action Center](/guides/action-center).

On the App Store, a Screenshot text column appears once the screenshots of the listing you look at, or of a listing that judges one of the keywords, were read. It marks the keywords a caption contains, reading the captions of the same listing that judged each keyword, and is labelled as a weak signal, because Apple has not confirmed that it reads screenshot text. It never changes whether a keyword counts as covered. See [Read screenshot captions](/guides/screenshot-captions).

Search the coverage table by keyword, sort it by keyword or bucket, and switch on **Uncovered only** to keep the keywords no indexed field contains.

To see what a new title or subtitle would cover before you ship it, [draft a listing](#draft-a-listing).

## Read your description

The description card counts words beside its characters. A word is a run of letters or digits between spaces, so `don't`, `e-mail` and `24/7` count once each, and links and email addresses count not at all. Japanese, Chinese, Thai, Lao, Khmer and Burmese text has no spaces, so the browser splits it into dictionary words, and the count there is an estimate that can differ slightly between browsers.

Under the text, **Word density** lists the words the description repeats most. A switch moves between **Words**, **Two words** and **Three words**, each showing its top 20. A word or phrase is listed only when it appears at least twice, a phrase never runs across a full stop, a comma or a line break, and phrases are hidden for text written mostly in Chinese or Japanese, where a dictionary split makes them noise. The table follows your edits as you type, keeps one height and scrolls when it holds more rows than fit.

The list leaves out links, numbers, words of one character, the words of your app's name and title, and common function words such as `the` and `with` for English and fifteen other languages: Spanish, Portuguese, French, German, Italian, Dutch, Swedish, Norwegian, Danish, Finnish, Polish, Turkish, Indonesian, Romanian and Czech. Store words such as free, best and new stay in, because those are repeats worth seeing. A word joined by a hyphen or a slash, such as `e-mail`, counts once in the total but is listed as its parts, the way keyword coverage reads it. Each row shows how often it appears and its share, the part of all the words of the description that the row takes up.

**Tracked** marks a row that is a keyword you track in this market. **Show in text** marks every place the row appears in a read only copy of the description, skipping links and addresses as the count does, and pressing it again hides the copy. A phrase that repeats back to back, such as `tab tab tab` for `tab tab`, counts each time it starts and is marked as one run.

On the App Store the description is not searched, so the table is about the people who read your page. On Google Play the description is searched, and the card says how many of your tracked keywords the live description holds, the same verdict as the description column of the [coverage table](#check-keyword-coverage), so the number follows the listing AsoBeast captured, not the text you are editing. The same section appears under the description of a [draft](#draft-a-listing).

## Draft a listing

Press **Draft** beside the **Localization** control to write the next version of the listing on screen beside the live one. The draft starts from the live title, subtitle and keyword field on the App Store, or the title and short description on Google Play, with the description folded under its own button. There is one draft per localization on the App Store and one per market on Google Play. It stays in this browser until you reset it with **Reset to live listing**. While you type, AsoBeast sends the draft text to its own server to judge coverage, and never sends it to the store. The URL keeps the mode as `?mode=draft`; the draft text never enters a link.

While you type, **Draft coverage** beside the fields judges every keyword you track in the markets that read the listing, with the same rules as [Keyword coverage](#check-keyword-coverage), so a draft that ships reads the same in the coverage table after the next capture. It lists the keywords the draft would no longer cover first, then the ones it would newly cover, the ones still not covered and the ones still covered, and words split count as covered on the App Store. Under them it lists the wasted words of the draft: a word that the title, subtitle or keyword field already holds, or a word in the subtitle, keyword field or short description that no keyword you track uses. Each field shows its lint, and screenshot captions are never counted. A market whose storefront reads the localization but that is still judged on your home listing, because its own listing has not been captured, is named instead of judged. When no market you track reads the localization, the panel says so.

The draft never saves the keyword field. **Review in the keyword field editor** opens the editor of that localization with the drafted field as unsaved text, and you save it there with the usual preview and choice of markets. While App Store Connect imports the field, the drafted field is read only and shows what the store serves. To move the rest of the draft to the store, **Copy for App Store Connect** or **Copy for Play Console** copies every field under the name the console form uses, and each field has its own **Copy** button.

## Pick target keywords

On the App Store, Draft mode opens with **Targets** for the localization you draft: what it should rank for. The first slot is meant for the title and the next two for the subtitle, on a tinted row, and **Show more slots** opens seven more. See [Target keywords of a localization](/concepts/countries#target-keywords-of-a-localization) for what a target is and where it is tracked.

Open a slot to pick one of the keywords your reader markets track, best opportunity first, with its popularity and position, or type a new phrase and choose **Track**. **Use top 3** fills the empty title and subtitle slots with the three highest opportunity keywords and never a competitor's brand, and keeps any slot you already filled. **Move up** and **Move down** swap a keyword with its neighbour from the keyboard, and the remove button empties a slot without moving the others. Nothing is stored until you press **Save targets**, and **Discard changes** returns to the saved list.

Saving tracks each new phrase in every market of your app that reads the localization, and a toast names what started being tracked. A phrase the keyword limit of your plan leaves out keeps its slot, a warning under the block names it and the market, and the slot says where it is not tracked. When a market that reads the localization joins later, **Track in N markets** tracks the saved targets there too.

Each target, and each keyword in a slot's list, shows where its scores suggest it belongs: **Title**, **Subtitle**, **Keyword field** or **Skip**. Press the suggestion to read why in one sentence, and press **Apply suggestions** to reorder the slots to match, an unsaved change you review and save. See [Where does each keyword belong?](/concepts/scoring#where-does-each-keyword-belong) for the rules.

**Draft coverage** lists the targets first, each with its slot and whether the draft covers it, and says **Was covered** or **Newly covered** when the draft changes that. A localization with targets shows them as soon as Draft mode opens.

## Preview the listing in the store

**In the store** draws the listing the way a phone shows it. The app overview shows the newest captured listing of the market on screen, and Draft mode shows your draft beside its fields and follows every keystroke. **Search result** draws the row a search lists: the icon, the title, the subtitle on the App Store or the developer on Google Play, the rating and, on the App Store, the first three screenshots. **Product page** draws the top of the product page: the title, the subtitle or the short description, the opening text and the screenshots. A switch picks a 6.1 inch or a 6.7 inch iPhone on the App Store, or a smaller or a larger phone on Google Play. The preview follows the light or dark theme of the page, and you can fold it on the overview.

When the title or the subtitle is longer than the phone can show, the preview cuts it with an ellipsis where the store would and says after which words, for example "Cut after “GeoGuess Map” on smaller iPhones." Words after the cut still count for search, but nobody reads them in the results. The cut is an estimate. On the App Store the type sizes and text widths follow the device preview in App Store Connect. AsoBeast measures the text with the store's own font where your device has it, which is the case in Safari or Chrome on a Mac, and with a close substitute elsewhere, and the store's rendering can still differ by a few pixels.

Under each draft field, the words search reads are listed as tags, split and normalized the way [Keyword coverage](#check-keyword-coverage) reads them. On the App Store a word the title, subtitle or keyword field already holds is read once, so its second use is marked **repeat** once the draft coverage answers. A field the store does not search, such as the description or the promotional text on the App Store, shows **Not searched** and no tags.

The store never shares promotional text with AsoBeast, so the overview shows the opening of the description in its place. Type promotional text in the draft to see it above the screenshots of the product page. It is not searched and never sent with the coverage check.

## Switch markets

When you track keywords in more than one market, a **Listing market** control sits above the fields. It lists your home storefront and every market whose listing AsoBeast has captured. Choose one to see the title, subtitle, description and screenshot captions that storefront serves, with the coverage of the keywords tracked there. The URL keeps the choice as `?market=`, so a link opens on the same market.

When the market has a native localization, a **Localization** control next to it shows that localization's fields and captions, and the URL keeps it as `?localization=`. A keyword covered only by a native localization says which one, for example in Title (Polish). See [Countries and markets](/concepts/countries#what-about-storefronts-that-default-to-english).

Each keyword is judged against the listing of its own market, and against the home listing until its market has one. The keyword field card shows the field you saved for the localization on screen, linted against that localization's title and subtitle, and the suggestion below the coverage table names the localization it is for. For an English localization the suggestion writes a word in its singular only when you track that singular too, `habit` for `habits`, so a word like `focus` or `news` keeps every letter; Apple matches the singular and the plural, so one form is enough. The AI drafts stay on the home market. The API takes the same choice as the `country` parameter of `GET /apps/{id}/metadata/audit`. See [What is a market listing?](/concepts/countries#what-is-a-market-listing)

## Edit the iOS keyword field

The 100 character field is private and cannot be collected, so you enter it here, once per localization. This is the only place that edits a keyword field; the keywords page reports what each market reads. See [See what each market reads](/guides/track-keywords#see-what-each-market-reads).

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the localization">
    Click its item in the **App Store localizations** section. The URL keeps the open item as `?fieldLocale=`, so an **Edit** link from the keywords page or the **Edit this keyword field** link on a suggestion opens the right one. An app with a single localization opens it for you.

    Arriving from **Add to keyword field** on the keywords page, the field already holds the phrases you selected as unsaved text, and a line above the localizations says how many were added, how many the field already held and how many did not fit. Nothing is stored until you save. When App Store Connect manages the field, or the app does not list that localization, the line says the phrases were not added and the field stays as it was.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Type or paste the field">
    Separate the keywords with commas. The counter shows the characters used of 100 and turns to a warning when you pass the limit, and **Save keyword field** stays disabled while the field is over the limit, or empty and untouched. Saving an unchanged field is how you track its phrases in a market that does not track them yet.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save and choose where to track">
    AsoBeast previews the save. If it adds and drops nothing, it saves at once and tracks in every market that reads the localization. Otherwise a dialog lists the new phrases, the phrases that leave the field and a checkbox for each of your markets that reads the localization, all ticked. It sums, for the ticked markets, the keyword markets the save adds and the store requests they cost each day, and on a plan with a keyword market limit it shows how many you will use after saving.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Save and track** tracks every phrase of the field, with the source `KEYWORD_FIELD`, in each ticked market. **Save** with nothing ticked stores the field and tracks nothing new, and every market that reads the localization still counts the field for coverage. Phrases that left the field stop being tracked as keyword field phrases in every market that reads the localization, whatever you tick, unless another field there holds them. A save that would pass your plan's keyword market limit is refused with nothing written, the field included, and the dialog links to the plans instead. See [How does AsoBeast keep a keyword field per localization?](/concepts/countries#how-does-asobeast-keep-a-keyword-field-per-localization)

Come back to the page and the field shows what AsoBeast tracks rather than the string you pasted. See [How do you read and write the keyword field?](/concepts/stores#how-do-you-read-and-write-the-keyword-field).

<Warning>
  Leaving the field empty is not neutral. Coverage reports every keyword as uncovered by the keyword field, because AsoBeast has no evidence about what it contains, and the audit scores keyword field hygiene as unset.
</Warning>

## When App Store Connect imports the field

The import is opt in per app. Connect App Store Connect as in [Connect App Store Connect](/integrations/app-store-connect), open the app's metadata page and press **Import from App Store Connect**. Only the workspace owner can start and stop the import. The dialog names the keyword fields that will be replaced, and warns that App Store Connect does not serve every localization, that the localizations it does not serve lose their fields here, and that stopping does not bring them back. From then on AsoBeast reads the private field of the live version through your key, once per localization, and tracks its phrases in every market whose storefront reads the localization as its default localization. The App Store changes keywords only with a new version, so AsoBeast imports again whenever it captures a new version of the listing, and **Sync now** in Integrations imports sooner. Other apps the key can see keep their fields untouched until you turn the import on for them too.

An imported field is read only. The editor shows what Apple serves and names the time of the last read, or says the import is waiting for its first read until one happens, **Add localization** leaves the section while the import follows, and a manual save through the API answers 409. Press **Stop importing** and the fields are yours to edit again exactly as App Store Connect last served them, without touching the funnel connection. A key that stops working pauses the import: the page says so, the fields are editable again, and they follow the store once the key works.

Five limits are worth knowing. The first import replaces hand typed fields, and the dialog names which. From that first import on the field follows App Store Connect, an emptied field included. A phrase AsoBeast cannot track, such as one with more than five words, is left out of the import. An import that would pass your plan's keyword market limit stops at the field it was writing, names that localization in the notice, and finishes on the next sync. And a localization App Store Connect stops serving loses its field here, the home localization excepted, together with the tracking of its phrases as keyword field phrases. An answer from App Store Connect that AsoBeast cannot read, such as an empty reply or a web page from a proxy in between, never counts as a localization it stopped serving: the fields stay as they were, the section says the last import stopped and why, and the next sync tries again. Recommendations the import makes obsolete, such as adding an uncovered keyword, close on the re-run that follows every import.

## Group keywords into buckets

Buckets classify scored keywords so a long list stays readable and so you can see whether your targeting is balanced. Their definitions live in [Buckets](/guides/track-keywords#buckets).

## Generate a metadata draft

With `OPENAI_API_KEY` set, a drafts card proposes title, subtitle and keyword field values on the App Store, or title, short description and description values on Google Play, grounded in your tracked keywords, your coverage gaps and competitor titles.

Every draft is linted again on the server and clamped to the field limit, so a draft can never exceed a store limit. AsoBeast has no write path to the stores: a draft ends at a copy button, or at **Use in draft**, which writes it into your [listing draft](#draft-a-listing) of the same localization and opens it.

With no key the card is hidden entirely. See [Enable the optional AI features](/guides/ai-features).

On an App Store app the drafts card has a **Localization** menu. **Primary listing** drafts the listing AsoBeast reads, as before. An additional language of one of your storefronts drafts that localization instead: its title, subtitle and keyword field, written in that language from the meaning of your tracked keywords rather than copied from them, preferring words your current listing does not use. A draft for a localization is told the keyword field you saved for it. The keyword field is held to its 100 character limit, and a Chinese, Japanese or Korean character counts once. See [How do you read and write the keyword field?](/concepts/stores#how-do-you-read-and-write-the-keyword-field)

Each localization item has a **Draft with AI** button that picks it in the menu and moves the focus there. To fill a localization's keyword field with keywords in another language, a common tactic for Spanish (Mexico) in the United States, say so in the instructions. Which storefronts read which localization is explained in [Which localizations does a storefront read?](/concepts/countries#which-localizations-does-a-storefront-read)

## Troubleshooting

- **A keyword you added to the title still shows as uncovered.** Coverage reads the latest snapshot. Refresh the listing after the store publishes the change.
- **The subtitle card is missing.** You are looking at a Google Play app. It has no subtitle.
- **Character counts differ from App Store Connect.** AsoBeast counts Unicode code points of the stored value, so a Chinese, Japanese or Korean character counts once even when it lies outside the Basic Multilingual Plane, and an emoji made of several parts, such as a flag or a family, counts once for each part. Apple does not document how App Store Connect counts those, so a field with emoji sequences can read differently there.
- **The Localization menu is missing.** The app is on Google Play, none of your storefronts lists an additional language (the United Kingdom lists none), or AsoBeast could not read your keyword markets when the page loaded.
- **Find localizations found nothing.** The store proves only a localization whose text differs from the default listing of the market it is asked in and from your home listing, and only for languages your home listing says the app supports. Add a localization with the default text by hand.
- **I track a market and see its listing but no keyword field for its language.** The localization is added after the next capture of that market. Press **Refresh** with that market chosen, or add it from **Not listed yet**.
- **A localization came back after I removed it.** **Find localizations** adds every localization the store serves again. Remove it once more after the run, or leave it: a localization the store serves is one your app ships.
