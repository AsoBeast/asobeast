The workbench is where you see your listing the way search sees it: field by field, against the limits, and against the keywords you claim to be targeting.

## Lint your metadata

Each field is checked against the limit for its store, and the lint reports characters used, characters left and anything wasteful such as a duplicated word across indexed fields.

| Field | App Store | Google Play |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Title | 30 | 30 |
| Subtitle | 30 | Not present |
| Keyword field | 100 | Not present |
| Short description | Not present | 80 |
| Description | 4000, not indexed | 4000, indexed |
| Promotional text | 170, not indexed | Not present |

Fields a store does not have are hidden rather than shown empty. See [App Store and Google Play](/concepts/stores).

On Google Play, a short description word that is also in the title is a warning, not an error. Google's metadata policy asks for succinct text without repeated keywords, but it does not forbid reusing a title word, so the lint points out the wasted room without calling it a rule break. On the App Store a subtitle or keyword field word that Apple already indexes from the title stays an error, and only errors open an Action Center item.

## See which localizations each storefront reads

On an App Store app, the card under the fields lists your home storefront first and then every storefront where you track keywords, each with Apple's default language and additional languages. A storefront with no additional language says so.

The fields above exist once per localization, so each additional language is another title, subtitle and keyword field for search in that storefront, and the card counts that room for you. The rule, its source and its limits are in [Which localizations does a storefront read?](/concepts/countries#which-localizations-does-a-storefront-read) The listing market control above the fields shows what each of those storefronts actually serves.

## Check keyword coverage

Coverage answers one question per tracked keyword: does any indexed field of this listing contain it?

A field contains a keyword when it has the keyword as whole words. Chinese, Japanese, Thai, Lao, Khmer and Burmese do not put spaces between words, so a keyword in one of those scripts counts wherever it appears in the field, and a character of those scripts ends a word written next to it as a space would. The subtitle `AIが3行要約` therefore contains `ai` and `iPhone用カメラ` contains `iphone`, while `SmartNewsアプリ` does not contain `news`. Korean does put spaces between words but attaches particles to their end, so a Korean keyword counts as a whole word or as a word followed by a particle: `운동` is found in `운동을`, but not in `운동화`, and `계부` is not found in `가계부`. A keyword that mixes scripts, such as `line 漫画`, counts when each of its words is found by its own rule.

| Store | Fields that count toward coverage |
| --- | --- |
| App Store | Title, subtitle, keyword field |
| Google Play | Title, short description, description |

A keyword with high opportunity and no coverage is the single most actionable state in the product, which is why the Action Center has a rule for exactly that. See [Work the Action Center](/guides/action-center).

On the App Store, a Screenshot text column appears once at least one screenshot caption of the listing you look at was read. It marks the keywords a caption contains, reading the captions of the same listing that judged each keyword, and is labelled as a weak signal, because Apple has not confirmed that it reads screenshot text. It never changes whether a keyword counts as covered. See [Read screenshot captions](/guides/screenshot-captions).

Search the coverage table by keyword, sort it by keyword or bucket, and switch on **Uncovered only** to keep the keywords no indexed field contains.

## Switch markets

When you track keywords in more than one market, a **Listing market** control sits above the fields. It lists your home storefront and every market whose listing asobeast has captured. Choose one to see the title, subtitle, description and screenshot captions that storefront serves, with the coverage of the keywords tracked there. The URL keeps the choice as `?market=`, so a link opens on the same market.

Each keyword is judged against the listing of its own market, and against the home listing until its market has one. The keyword field and the AI drafts stay on the home market, because the keyword field is private and you paste it once, for the home storefront. The API takes the same choice as the `country` parameter of `GET /apps/{id}/metadata/audit`. See [What is a market listing?](/concepts/countries#what-is-a-market-listing)

## Paste the iOS keyword field

The 100 byte field is private and cannot be collected, so paste it here. asobeast splits it, tracks the keywords with the source `KEYWORD_FIELD` and lints the field against its limit.

Come back to the page and the field shows what asobeast tracks rather than the string you pasted. See [How do you read and write the keyword field?](/concepts/stores#how-do-you-read-and-write-the-keyword-field).

<Warning>
  Leaving the field empty is not neutral. Coverage reports every keyword as uncovered by the keyword field, because asobeast has no evidence about what it contains, and the audit scores keyword field hygiene as unset.
</Warning>

## Group keywords into buckets

Buckets classify scored keywords so a long list stays readable and so you can see whether your targeting is balanced.

| Bucket | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| `primary` | The five highest opportunity keywords that are not aspirational |
| `aspirational` | Volume at or above 70 and difficulty at or above 70 |
| `longtail` | Three words or more, or volume below 30 |
| `secondary` | Every other scored keyword |

A listing whose indexed fields cover only aspirational keywords will rank for none of them. A listing that covers only longtail will rank for many things nobody searches. The buckets make that visible in one glance.

## Generate a metadata draft

With `OPENAI_API_KEY` set, a drafts card proposes title, subtitle and keyword field values on the App Store, or title, short description and description values on Google Play, grounded in your tracked keywords, your coverage gaps and competitor titles.

Every draft is linted again on the server and clamped to the field limit, so a draft can never exceed a store limit. asobeast has no write path to the stores: a draft ends at a copy button.

With no key the card is hidden entirely. See [Enable the optional AI features](/guides/ai-features).

On an App Store app the drafts card has a **Localization** menu. **Primary listing** drafts the listing asobeast reads, as before. An additional language of one of your storefronts drafts that localization instead: its title, subtitle and keyword field, written in that language from the meaning of your tracked keywords rather than copied from them, preferring words your current listing does not use. The keyword field is held to its 100 byte limit, which a Chinese, Japanese or Korean draft reaches in about 33 characters. See [How do you read and write the keyword field?](/concepts/stores#how-do-you-read-and-write-the-keyword-field)

Each additional language on the storefront card has a button that picks it in the menu and moves the focus there. To fill a localization's keyword field with keywords in another language, a common tactic for Spanish (Mexico) in the United States, say so in the instructions. Which storefronts read which localization is explained in [Which localizations does a storefront read?](/concepts/countries#which-localizations-does-a-storefront-read)

## Troubleshooting

- **A keyword you added to the title still shows as uncovered.** Coverage reads the latest snapshot. Refresh the listing after the store publishes the change.
- **The subtitle card is missing.** You are looking at a Google Play app. It has no subtitle.
- **Character counts differ from App Store Connect.** asobeast counts characters, so an emoji or an accented character counts as the store counts it in the stored value.
- **The Localization menu is missing.** The app is on Google Play, none of your storefronts lists an additional language (the United Kingdom lists none), or asobeast could not read your keyword markets when the page loaded.
