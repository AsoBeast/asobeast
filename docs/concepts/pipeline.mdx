Collection is scheduled, not on demand. A daily cron enqueues every position check, snapshot, review sync and category rank, and two rate limited workers drain those queues over the following hours.

## What runs and when?

Every schedule is a cron expression evaluated in UTC. The defaults below come from `apps/api/.env.example`.

| Variable | Default | Meaning | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `CRON_DAILY` | `0 3 * * *` | Daily 03:00 | Enqueues position checks, snapshots, reviews and category ranks, then generates actions and flushes batched alerts |
| `CRON_SCORING` | `0 4 * * 0` | Sunday 04:00 | Recomputes popularity and difficulty for tracked keywords |
| `CRON_RETENTION` | `0 5 * * *` | Daily 05:00 | Prunes rows past their retention window |
| `CRON_STORE_REPORTS` | `30 5 * * *` | Daily 05:30 | Downloads the reports each connected store console published since the last sync. Never scheduled without `STORE_CONNECT_ENCRYPTION_KEY` |
| `CRON_AUDIT` | `0 6 * * *` | Daily 06:00 | Writes the daily audit score snapshot |
| `CRON_LOCALIZATION_SWEEP` | `0 7 * * 1` | Monday 07:00 | Queues the lookups that detect the App Store localizations every app ships. An empty value removes it |
| `CRON_DIGEST` | `0 8 * * 1` | Monday 08:00 | Sends the weekly digest webhook |

Batched alerts do not have their own schedule. They send once the daily pipeline reports every child job settled, so the delivery time depends on workload and retries rather than on a fixed clock.

## What are the two workers?

| Worker | Concurrency | Limiter | Default |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `appstore` | 1 | `SCRAPE_ITUNES_RPM` requests per minute | 15 |
| `gplay` | 1 | `SCRAPE_GPLAY_RPM` job starts per minute | 10 |

The difference matters. The Apple limiter spaces requests, while the Google Play limiter spaces job starts, because one Google Play job fans out into many sequential requests of its own.

Work a person starts to explore rather than to collect, today the lookups of a deep search, is queued with a priority. A worker always takes every waiting job without a priority first, the daily run's jobs among them, so a deep search waits behind them. Such a lookup also checks, before it reaches the store, that its workspace still has a plan in force and is not suspended, and does nothing otherwise.

Reading screenshot captions runs in a third queue, `screenshots`, with its own worker at concurrency 1. While reading is on, every snapshot that records App Store screenshots queues one read, a market listing refresh included. With `SCREENSHOT_OCR=false` the screenshots are recorded as skipped and nothing is queued. It spends no store request budget and no OpenAI call. See [Read screenshot captions](/guides/screenshot-captions).

Downloading store console reports runs in a fourth queue, `store-connect`, with its own worker at concurrency 1. It talks to App Store Connect with the key an owner connected, not to the public store, so it spends no store request budget and no proxy. A sync job per workspace is queued by `CRON_STORE_REPORTS` and by the connect, verify and sync buttons, and one already waiting is not queued twice. See [Read impressions and conversion from your console](/guides/store-funnel).

## Why are the limits conservative?

The iTunes endpoints informally tolerate roughly 20 requests per minute per IP address. Google Play is more sensitive still. Neither store publishes a limit or an error you can negotiate with, so asobeast stays well underneath by default.

Raising `SCRAPE_ITUNES_RPM` or `SCRAPE_GPLAY_RPM` puts the host IP address at risk of being throttled or blocked by the store, which stops collection for every app on that host. Do not point several instances at the stores from one IP address.

## How do you estimate your daily budget?

Daily request volume is roughly tracked keywords multiplied by tracked markets, plus per app work for snapshots, reviews and category ranks, and one listing refresh per app, competitor and extra market.

| Job | Approximate cost |
| --- | --- |
| Apple position check at depth 200 | 1 request |
| Google Play position check at depth 200 | Roughly 8 requests |
| Apple score job | 3 to 11 requests |
| Google Play score job | 12 to 20 requests |
| Category ranks per app | The free, paid and grossing charts of its category and of the overall chart, each one request a day captured once for the whole instance |
| Followed category | Three charts a day, shared with any app or workspace already in them |
| Market listing refresh | One job per app and market, the same weight as an app refresh |
| Localization read | One more listing read per native localization of a storefront and per localization your app ships that the storefront reads besides its default |

`GET /jobs/budget` returns an estimate broken down by kind, and the settings page renders it as a budget card with a utilization meter. The dashboard warns when the daily pipeline would exceed 85 percent of capacity. Both are [shown only to the platform operator](/security/admin-surfaces#views-only-the-operator-sees).

Category charts are the smallest slice of the daily budget, but they count, because the daily run captures every chart kind of each category an app sits in and of the overall chart, and every followed category adds its three charts. A chart is fetched once a day however many apps and workspaces sit in it. See [Read top charts](/guides/top-charts).

## Can you call the store outside the queue?

No, with two exceptions. All collection goes through the queue so the limiters apply. The exceptions are a small, user initiated suggestion lookup and a store lookup when you search a store by name or ask for similar apps, both bounded by a person clicking rather than by a schedule, and by their hourly allowances.

## What is retained?

The retention job prunes each table on its own window. `0` means keep forever. Every variable is described in the [configuration reference](/configuration/reference).

| Variable | Default | Prunes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `RETENTION_RANKINGS_DAYS` | 365 | Keyword positions |
| `RETENTION_SERP_DAYS` | 90 | SERP entries |
| `RETENTION_SNAPSHOTS_DAYS` | 180 | App snapshots, keeping the newest per app and market |
| `RETENTION_CATEGORY_RANKS_DAYS` | 365 | Category ranks |
| `RETENTION_CHARTS_DAYS` | 365 | Top chart captures |
| `RETENTION_CHANGE_EVENTS_DAYS` | 0 | Metadata change events |
| `RETENTION_DELIVERIES_DAYS` | 30 | Alert delivery log rows |
| `RETENTION_ALERT_EVENTS_DAYS` | 30 | Completed alert claims, keeping unfinished ones |
| `RETENTION_AUDIT_SCORES_DAYS` | 0 | Audit score history |
| `RETENTION_ACTIONS_DAYS` | 180 | Closed action items only |
| `RETENTION_AI_CALLS_DAYS` | 400 | AI call ledger rows, never from the current month |
| `RETENTION_STORE_REPORTS_DAYS` | 35 | Downloaded store console report files. The funnel rows built from them are kept |

Three exceptions are worth remembering. The newest snapshot per app is always kept regardless of age, open or snoozed action items are never pruned by age, and AI calls from the current month are never pruned because the monthly AI allowance still counts them. The same job also gives back AI calls that were reserved more than an hour ago and never settled.

## What happens when a job fails?

A parse failure fails the job. BullMQ retries it with backoff, and request handling is never affected, so a broken parser degrades collection rather than taking the instance down.

An App Store request that stops answering is abandoned after 15 seconds and retried like any other failure, so one slow connection cannot hold the `appstore` worker. Google Play requests have their own 30 second bound per attempt.

An empty answer is not trusted when the same question had an answer within the last seven days: a search for a keyword that ranked apps, or a review feed for an app that was receiving reviews. On the App Store the job asks again three times, four seconds apart, and then fails its attempt so the queue retries it, because Apple's origin sometimes answers empty for a moment. App Store searches, lookups, charts and review feeds always reach Apple's origin rather than a copy cached at Apple's edge, so each retry sees a fresh answer. On Google Play the same empty answer is a soft block, and the job fails at once without retrying.

Top charts follow the same rule, with the job's attempts as the second look. A chart whose apps were last listed within seven days and that answers empty fails every attempt, because that is how a blocked request looks. Any other chart that answers empty is asked again on each attempt and stored as a capture with no rows only after the last one fails to find an app, so one blocked request on the first day of a new chart is never stored as an empty chart. The next day the chart is already known to be empty, is stored on the first attempt, and costs one request a day and no failed job. See [What does an empty chart mean?](/guides/top-charts#what-does-an-empty-chart-mean)

Failed jobs are visible on the queue dashboard at `/admin/queues`, which answers only to the [platform operator](/security/admin-surfaces#who-the-platform-operator-is). A failed chart job left by an earlier version stays in the failed count, and the header badge keeps reading degraded, until the operator cleans it on the dashboard. See [Troubleshooting](/operations/troubleshooting).

## How do you follow today's run for one app?

Every app page shows a pill beside **Run daily** with where today's run of that app stands: how many of its listings, position checks and chart reads are in, out of how many the day needs. A chart read is a rank row of the app: the chart of its price class and the grossing chart, each in its category and overall, so an app counts four, not the six charts the daily run captures for everyone. Open it for one row per stage and the time the run started. It counts what landed since the daily trigger, or since midnight UTC once the trigger has passed, says **Run complete** when every count is in and **Run delayed** when the run started over twenty hours ago with counts still missing. A workspace the daily run skips, because an operator suspended it or its plan is no longer in force, shows no pill and is not polled, and `GET /apps/{id}/run-status` answers `paused: true` next to the unchanged counts. A run that finished before the pause still reads **Run complete**. See [Rate limits](/api-reference/rate-limits) for suspension and [Billing](/operations/billing) for plans. While the run is collecting it asks again every ten seconds and refreshes the tables as positions land, so **Run daily** is followed by visible progress. The same answer is available as `GET /apps/{id}/run-status` and as the `app_run_status` MCP tool, computed from stored rows and never from the queue. Reviews are not counted, because a sync that finds nothing new leaves no trace.

## What does Run daily do after a failure?

Run daily collects today's work again for one app. A job that failed earlier today, because a store was down or blocked, is queued again with a fresh set of attempts, and its failed record leaves the dashboard. A job that is still waiting, running or finished today is left alone, so a second click does not queue it again.

The response counts only what the click queued. When every count is zero, today's checks were already queued, running or done, and the click still uses one of the plan's on demand Run daily credits. Requests that reach the API at the same moment queue each job once, and at most one of them counts it, even when they replace a job that failed or carry the same request id.
