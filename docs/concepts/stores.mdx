Both stores are live and equally supported. What differs is which fields each store indexes for search, and one of Apple's indexed fields cannot be read from the public listing at all.

On the App Store, asobeast reads iPhone search, so it tracks listings that are available on iPhone. An app sold only on iPad, the Mac App Store, Apple Watch or Apple Vision Pro never appears in that search, so it is refused on import and as a competitor. Apple keeps a separate iPad catalogue that lists iPad only apps, and asobeast does not read it. An app imported before this rule shows a notice on its pages, because its positions cannot be found.

## What does each store index?

| Field | App Store limit | Google Play limit | Indexed for search | Readable from the listing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Title | 30 | 30 | Both stores | Yes |
| Subtitle | 30 | Not present | App Store | Yes |
| Keyword field | 100 | Not present | App Store | No |
| Short description | Not present | 80 | Google Play | Yes |
| Long description | 4000, not indexed | 4000, indexed | Google Play | Yes |
| Promotional text | 170, not indexed | Not present | Neither | Yes |

Google Play search therefore reads three surfaces: the title, the 80 character short description and the 4000 character long description. There is no subtitle and no keyword field on Google Play, so those two stay Apple only concepts everywhere in asobeast, including the types, the metadata lints and the audit rubric.

In a storefront that defaults to English, asobeast also captures the native localization. See [Countries and markets](/concepts/countries#what-about-storefronts-that-default-to-english).

In practice, App Store search combines the words of the title, subtitle and keyword field into phrases within one localization, never across two, and the order of the words does not matter. Apple does not document either behaviour; both are the consistent observation of ASO practitioners.

## Is screenshot text indexed?

Apple has not documented screenshot text as an indexed field, and Google has not either. The App Store column of the table above therefore lists three fields, not four.

Since early June 2025, ASO practitioners have reported that apps rank for words that appear only in their screenshot captions on the App Store. Appfigures measured it first, and Phiture and AppTweak repeated the report. Apple has been reported to deny it, and the one controlled test published, by ConsultMyApp, found 1 of 64 caption phrases ranking without an explanation in the title, subtitle or keyword field. asobeast treats screenshot text as a weak, unconfirmed signal: it reads it on the App Store, shows it beside keyword coverage and records its changes, but never counts it as an indexed field. Google Play screenshots are not read at all. See [Read screenshot captions](/guides/screenshot-captions).

## Why is the iOS keyword field private?

The 100 byte iOS keyword field never appears on the public store page, so no amount of collection can read it. It exists only in App Store Connect.

asobeast handles this by asking you for it. Paste the field in the metadata workbench and asobeast stores those keywords with the source `KEYWORD_FIELD`, which lets coverage checks, the audit rubric and the Action Center reason about a surface they cannot otherwise see.

Leaving it empty is not neutral. Coverage will report the field as uncovered because asobeast has no evidence that it contains anything.

## How do you read and write the keyword field?

The field is one endpoint pair on the app, and both answer the same `KeywordFieldResult` shape.

| Operation | Endpoint |
| --- | --- |
| Submit the field | `PUT /apps/{id}/keyword-field` |
| Read the stored field | `GET /apps/{id}/keyword-field` |

Six things the schema cannot tell you.

- **The value is the tracked set, not your string.** asobeast normalizes each phrase, drops the empties and removes the duplicates before it tracks anything, so `Focus Timer, POMODORO ,pomodoro` reads back as `focus timer,pomodoro`. Spacing and casing can differ from what you submitted, a repeated phrase stays where it first appeared, and the byte count is the count of the normalized set. Accented letters are stored in their composed form, so `zażółć` pasted as decomposed text, which macOS copy and paste can produce, is tracked and counted the same as `zażółć` typed directly. The phrases keep the order of the latest submission.
- **A save is held to the 100 byte limit.** App Store Connect counts the keyword field in UTF-8 bytes, so a letter such as ą takes 2 and a Chinese, Japanese or Korean character takes 3. The limit is measured on the normalized set, the same count `charactersUsed` reports in bytes, so spacing and duplicates in the submitted text do not count against it. A save whose normalized set runs past 100 bytes answers 400 and changes nothing.
- **It belongs to the home market.** The read takes no country and scopes itself to `app.country`, because the keyword field belongs to the app rather than to a storefront. See [Countries and markets](/concepts/countries).
- **`duplicatesRemoved` is 0 on a read.** It reports what the last submission discarded, and a read submits nothing.
- **A save never untracks a keyword something else tracks.** A phrase already tracked from the title, the subtitle, the short description or a manual add joins the field without losing that source, and the API reports it as `KEYWORD_FIELD` while it is in the field. A later save that leaves it out only takes it out of the field, so it stays tracked under its own source. A phrase that only the field tracks is deactivated when a save leaves it out. Adding such a phrase manually or reactivating it makes it a manual keyword, and a snapshot that finds it in the title, subtitle or short description gives it that source.
- **The last save wins.** Saves to one app are written one at a time, so two saves that overlap never merge: the field holds the phrases of whichever save was written last. Each save answers with the field as it was stored when that save finished, so a save that overlapped another may be answered with the other save's phrases.

An app that has never had a keyword field reads as an empty result with 0 characters used rather than a 404, because an empty field is a normal state. A Google Play app refuses both operations with 400.

## Why is the Google Play short description its indexed surface?

The 80 character short description, called `summary` in the store payload, is Google Play's equivalent of the surfaces Apple splits across a subtitle and a keyword field. asobeast treats it accordingly.

- Its keywords are tracked automatically with the source `DESCRIPTION`.
- The metadata workbench lints it against the 80 character limit.
- Keyword coverage counts it as an indexed field.

The store page escapes some characters in the short description, so `Math & Music` arrives as `Math &amp; Music`. asobeast stores the short description as the plain text a user reads, keeps the payload exactly as received in `raw`, and counts characters the way Google Play does. An upgrade decodes short descriptions already stored and pauses the auto tracked keywords an escape produced, such as `amp music`, keeping their history.

## How are auto tracked keywords chosen?

On import and on every refresh, asobeast reads the words of the title and of the subtitle (App Store) or the short description (Google Play), and tracks up to 15 phrases of one to three words. Title phrases come first, then longer phrases before shorter ones. Stopwords such as app, free and best are skipped, and so are words of one character.

A word with an apostrophe is read as the word it contracts. The ending of `you've`, `it's`, `we'll` and `I'm` is dropped, so `RPG you've been waiting for!` gives `rpg waiting` and never `ve`, and a possessive such as `Kid's Games` gives `kid games`. A negated auxiliary such as `don't` or `can't` is dropped like a stopword. A number written with a separator between its digits, such as `10,000`, `1.000`, `2.0`, `24/7`, `10:30`, or `10 000` with a non breaking space, is a quantity and not a keyword. It is dropped together with a unit of up to three letters written right after it, as in `3.5mm` or `2.4GHz`, and ends the phrase before it, so `10,000+ magazines in one app` never gives `10` or `000`. A longer word written right after it stays a keyword, so `10,000Songs` gives `songs`. A number that is part of a word keeps that word, so `Web3.0` gives `web3`. A number without a separator, such as `2048`, `3D` or `100+`, is kept. A plain space between digit groups is not read as a separator, so `10 000` typed with a plain space still gives `10` and `000`.

Japanese, Chinese, Thai, Lao, Khmer and Burmese have no spaces between words, so asobeast splits them with the word segmenter that ships with the runtime, `Intl.Segmenter`, and joins the words of a phrase without spaces, the way a person types it in the store.

- **In these scripts a phrase never crosses a space, a dash or a punctuation mark.** `メルカリ` and `フリマアプリ` in a title are two keywords, never one joined keyword.
- **Store noise words at the start and end of a run are removed first.** `無料アプリ` gives no keyword at all, and `無料ゲーム` gives `ゲーム`.
- **A hiragana run of five characters or fewer stays whole.** The segmenter cuts hiragana into pieces it cannot tell from grammar, so `ふりま` and `ぐるなび` are kept as one keyword each, and `しまむら公式アプリ` gives `しまむら`. Any other short run is split like a long one, so `天気予報` gives `天気予報`, `天気` and `予報`, and `猫のゲーム` gives only `ゲーム`. A short Chinese title with a word such as `我的` in it is kept whole as well, so `我的世界` is a keyword next to `世界`.
- **A grammar word ends a phrase.** Hiragana only fragments, single character Chinese particles such as `的` and `和`, and a short list of Japanese, Chinese and Thai noise words are dropped, and a phrase is never built across them. The Chinese particles apply only to text without kana, so a Japanese title keeps `東京都` and `就活`. A word written in hiragana inside a longer run is dropped with them, because the segmenter cannot tell it from grammar, so `ひまつぶしゲーム` gives only `ゲーム`.
- **Thai is split with a word list first.** The segmenter cuts many Thai words into syllables, so `สนามบิน` (airport) would come out as `สนาม` and `บิน` and a phrase could start or end inside a word. asobeast splits Thai with the word list of the PyThaiNLP project, about 62,000 words dedicated to the public domain under CC0, together with a few store words the list lacks, such as `แอป`, `ช้อป` and `เดลิเวอรี่`. The segmenter only splits the text the list does not know. A compound the list knows is one keyword, so `แอปสั่งอาหาร` gives `สั่งอาหาร` and not `สั่ง` and `อาหาร`.
- **A Thai function word ends a phrase, and a Thai prefix starts a word.** Words such as `ด้วย`, `ไป` and `กับ` are dropped like stopwords, and `การ`, `ความ`, `นัก` and `ผู้` join the word after them. So `จองการเดินทางไปสนามบินล่วงหน้า` gives the phrases `จองการเดินทาง` and `สนามบินล่วงหน้า` and the words `จอง`, `การเดินทาง`, `สนามบิน` and `ล่วงหน้า`, and never `จองการเดิน` or `สนามบินล่วง`.
- **A single Chinese or Japanese character stays with its neighbours.** Two or more in a row form one word, as in `畅聊`. A lone one never stands alone and can end a phrase, so `家計簿` is kept and `簿` is not offered. It starts a phrase only at the very start of a run, as in `新機能` and `筋トレ`, and not after a Latin word, so `iPhone用カメラ` gives `カメラ`. Brand names the segmenter cuts into single characters are joined back this way, so `微信`, `淘宝` and `小红书` stay whole.
- **A phrase of two or three words is at most 10 Chinese or Japanese characters, or 20 letters in Thai, Lao, Khmer and Burmese.** A letter counts together with its vowel and tone marks, so `สั่งอาหารออนไลน์` is a phrase. A single word is never cut, so a long loanword stays whole.
- **A katakana word that the segmenter splits is joined again.** A piece of one or two characters, or one that starts with a small kana, `ン` or `ー`, joins the katakana before it. A piece that ends in `ッ`, a two character piece that ends in `ー`, a single character at the start of a katakana run, and a piece joined from such pieces that is still two characters or fewer join the katakana after them. So `スマホ` is not offered as `スマ`, `ネットフリックス` and `ユーチューブ` stay whole, and `プリティーダービー` gives `プリティー` and `ダービー` and never `プリ` or `ティー`. A two character word the segmenter returns whole, such as `ヨガ` in `ヨガレッスン`, stays a keyword of its own.
- **Latin words in the same listing form phrases as usual.** A Latin brand next to Japanese text is its own phrase.

Neither the segmenter nor the word list knows every word. A brand written in Thai letters still comes out in syllables, so `ลาซาด้า` gives `ลา` and `ซา` next to `ลาซาด้า`, and a katakana word cut into pieces of two characters and more, such as `ダンジョン` or `ソーセージレジェンド`, comes out in pieces too. Pause such a fragment on the keyword monitor.

Arabic is written with spaces, but a conjunction and an article attach to the word after them. asobeast drops the noise words it knows, and also the same word after the conjunction `و`, so `وأكثر` goes with `أكثر`. It keeps every other word that begins with `و`, because many Arabic words do, such as `وقت` and `واتساب`, and it never strips the article `ال`. A keyword like `وطعام` therefore stays as it was written. The tatweel and the short vowel marks are ignored when a word is compared with the noise words, so `وأكـثر` and `أَكْثَر` are dropped as well, and a keyword that stays keeps the marks it was written with.

Korean keeps its spaces, so a particle attached to a word, as in `메신저를`, also stays attached. asobeast has no morphological analysis for Korean or Arabic.

The metadata lints, the ASO audit, the review phrases and the keyword combinations card still read words between spaces, so on a Japanese, Chinese or Thai listing they treat a run of text as one word. The audit can therefore call a keyword missing from the title that the coverage table shows as covered. Keyword coverage, on the metadata workbench and on the overview, and keyword relevance find such a keyword anywhere in the listing, so a keyword tracked from a listing counts as covered by it and earns its full relevance. See [Check keyword coverage](/guides/metadata-workbench#check-keyword-coverage) and [Where does relevance start?](/concepts/scoring#where-does-relevance-start)

An upgrade does not change keywords already tracked. The next refresh of an app, which the daily run performs, adds the cleaner keywords next to the sentences an earlier version tracked, and those stay active until you pause or remove them on the keyword monitor.

## What stays Apple only?

The subtitle and the keyword field. On a Google Play app they carry audit weight 0 and are hidden in the web app rather than shown empty, and the Action Center skips rules that would recommend an impossible edit.

## Are scores comparable across stores?

No. Popularity and difficulty are derived from different public evidence on each store, so an Apple difficulty of 60 and a Google Play difficulty of 60 do not mean the same thing.

Compare a keyword against other keywords in the same store. See [Popularity, difficulty and opportunity](/concepts/scoring).

## Where else do the stores differ?

| Difference | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Neither store's suggest endpoint carries a popularity value | On Google Play asobeast probes how early the store suggests the keyword; on the App Store it estimates popularity from the first 25 search results. See [Popularity, difficulty and opportunity](/concepts/scoring#how-is-popularity-estimated) |
| Google Play search results carry no rating counts | SERP rating columns stay empty for Google Play |
| The App Store publishes no install count | `installs` stays empty for App Store apps and their competitors |
| Google Play release dates are best effort outside the `en` storefront | Treat `released` as approximate there |
| Some Google Play apps publish a different build per device | Google Play reports their version as `VARY`. asobeast stores no version for them, so the overview and the competitor list show none and a refresh records no version change while the app keeps varying. When the app later reports a real version, the change reads from no version to that version. The payload in `raw` keeps `VARY`, and an upgrade clears the marker from snapshots and version changes already stored, dropping a stored version change that only moved between no version and `VARY` |
| Google Play reads a bare `zh` as Simplified Chinese | asobeast asks for Taiwan as `zh-TW` and for Hong Kong as `zh-HK`, so the stored title, short description and description, the keywords tracked from them, rank checks and suggestions all use the Traditional Chinese listing people there see. Every other storefront is requested in its own language, or in English where asobeast has none. An app imported before the change keeps its Simplified listing until its next refresh, which records one title change, a What's New change when the app has release notes, and one `metadata.changed` alert for apps whose home storefront is Taiwan or Hong Kong. Keywords already tracked from the Simplified text stay tracked, so untrack the ones you do not want. Rank history in these two storefronts steps once, because the store now ranks a different language |
| An App Store product page without a subtitle shows one of the app's categories in its place | asobeast reads a subtitle equal to any category of the listing as no subtitle: the primary category for most apps and a subcategory such as Action for a game. The category never counts toward coverage, is never tracked as a keyword, and a refresh records no subtitle change for it. A real subtitle that is exactly a category name is read the same way, because the page cannot tell the two apart. An upgrade clears these subtitles from snapshots already stored and from each side of a stored subtitle change that was read from such a snapshot, drops a stored subtitle change that only moved between no subtitle and a category, and stops tracking the keywords asobeast added from a category unless they are paused, sit in the keyword field, carry tags, a note or a relevance, or also appear in the title or subtitle |

## What happens when a store changes its response?

A parser breaks, for every installation at once. Only the provider layer parses store responses, so the break is one module, failed jobs stay contained, and no history is lost. See [Recover from a broken store parser](/operations/scraper-breakage).

## Why does a Google Play job cost more?

Because one logical job fans out to many requests. A Google Play score job makes 12 to 20 sequential requests: one search, one to nine suggest probes and ten detail enrichments. An Apple score job makes 3 to 11: one search, one to nine suggest probes and one lookup of the phrase followed by a space, which counts the searches that continue it. A keyword the store never suggests costs a single probe, plus that lookup on the App Store. A depth 200 Google Play position check takes roughly eight requests against one for Apple.

That is why `SCRAPE_GPLAY_RPM` spaces job starts rather than individual requests, and why its default of 10 is lower than the Apple default of 15. See [The daily pipeline and rate limits](/concepts/pipeline).
