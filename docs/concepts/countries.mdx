You import an app once. The app row carries one home storefront, and every tracked keyword carries its own country, so a single app tracks keywords across as many markets as you want.

## What is the home storefront?

The home storefront is `app.country`, and it comes from the URL you imported. A URL such as `apps.apple.com/de/app/...` imports a `de` home market, and the import dialog lets you override it. A bare iTunes id, or a URL with no country segment, falls back to `DEFAULT_COUNTRY`, which defaults to `us`.

Country codes are two lowercase letters, and each one must be a storefront of the app's store. See [Which codes are storefronts?](#which-codes-are-storefronts)

The home storefront drives everything that asobeast stores once per app.

| Stays on the home market | Per market |
| --- | --- |
| Category ranks | Tracked keywords |
| Reviews and ratings history | Positions and SERP snapshots |
| The iOS keyword field | Popularity and difficulty scores |
| Auto tracked keywords from the listing | Market comparison in the Action Center |
|  | Listings: metadata snapshots and their changes |

A keyword is judged against the listing its own storefront serves. See [What is a market listing?](#what-is-a-market-listing)

## How does one search serve everyone?

A position check searches `keyword.country`, not `app.country`. From that single search asobeast records positions for the primary app and for every competitor attached to it in that storefront.

That is why adding a competitor costs no extra store requests. Ten competitors on a keyword are still one search.

## What does a market cost?

The same phrase tracked in `us` and in `pl` is two keyword rows, checked by two searches. Markets multiply, keywords multiply, and both draw on the same fixed request budget.

Rankings, difficulty and popularity all differ per storefront, so the second market is real data rather than a copy.

Two places show you the cost before it bites.

- The settings page shows the [platform operator](/security/admin-surfaces#who-the-platform-operator-is) a daily request budget card with the estimated requests broken down by kind, the capacity and a utilization meter.
- `GET /jobs/budget` returns the same estimate, and the dashboard warns the platform operator when the daily pipeline would exceed 85 percent of capacity.

Each market with keywords also adds one listing refresh per app and competitor to the daily run, and the budget counts them: `GET /jobs/budget` reports them as `marketListings`, a part of `apps`.

When you are over budget, remove keywords or markets, or raise `SCRAPE_ITUNES_RPM` and accept the risk. See [The daily pipeline and rate limits](/concepts/pipeline).

## What is a market listing?

A store serves a different listing in each storefront. The App Store returns the title, the description and the rating count of the storefront you ask, so the same app can have a German title, a Japanese title and a Brazilian one, and a rating count that differs in each. asobeast captures that listing, as its own snapshot, for every market where you track at least one keyword, for your app and for each of its competitors.

| You | asobeast |
| --- | --- |
| Track a keyword in a new market, or resume a paused one there | asks for the listing of that market at once, for the app and its competitors, unless it already has one |
| Do nothing | refreshes each market listing in the daily run, as one job on the same store budget |
| Click Refresh while a market is selected | refreshes that market's listing and shows what changed |

A keyword is then judged against the listing of its own market: its coverage on the metadata page and its relevance next to the opportunity score. Until a market has a listing, a keyword is judged against the home listing, as every keyword was before.

Some things are still read once, from the home storefront, because they need more than the listing: category ranks, reviews and the ratings history, the iOS keyword field and the keywords asobeast tracks for you from your listing. The Action Center rules that judge coverage, lint and staleness also read the home listing. See [Action Center](/guides/action-center).

The Apple keyword field is private and belongs to the home storefront, so a keyword judged against a market listing is covered by its title and subtitle only. A keyword whose market has no listing yet is judged against the whole home listing, keyword field included.

A market whose keywords you all removed keeps its last listing readable in the switcher, but asobeast stops refreshing it, and the Refresh button says why instead of refreshing.

If an app is not published in a market, asobeast tries again the next day and stores nothing, so a market with keywords and no listing simply shows no switcher entry.

## How do you add a market?

On the keyword monitor, switch markets with the country filter, which shows the keyword count per market, then add keywords into the market you selected. A market with no keywords yet shows an empty state with an add button. The first keyword you add there asks for the market's listing, so the market appears in the listing market switcher after the next job runs.

## Which codes are storefronts?

A home storefront and every keyword market must be a place where the app's store operates. asobeast checks the code against the store before it reaches it, so a code that is not a storefront is refused at once instead of failing every job it would have queued.

| Store | Storefronts | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| App Store | 175 | [App Store pricing and availability start times by country or region](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/pricing-and-availability/app-store-pricing-and-availability-start-times-by-country-or-region/) |
| Google Play | 225 | [Supported locations for distribution to Google Play users](https://play.google.com/supported-locations?hl=en) |

The two lists differ, so the check is made per store. `pw` is an App Store storefront that Google Play does not list, and 51 codes such as `ad`, `bd` and `li` are Google Play locations without an App Store storefront. Kosovo is `xk` in both stores.

The refusal names the store, for example `zz is not an App Store storefront` or `pw is not a Google Play location`. It applies to importing an app, adding keywords, reactivating a paused keyword, deep search, keyword suggestions and the market availability check. A store URL whose country segment or `gl` parameter names no storefront is refused the same way.

An upgrade pauses any keyword that was already tracked in a code that is not a storefront. Its history stays, and it stops producing failed jobs.

## Which localizations does a storefront read?

Apple publishes, for every App Store storefront, a default language and the additional languages it supports, in [App Store localizations](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-store-localizations/). asobeast carries that table unchanged, for the same 175 storefronts it accepts.

| From Apple's table | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Localizations a listing can have | 50 |
| Storefronts with at least one additional language | 104 |
| Storefronts with English (U.K.) and nothing else | 71 |
| Additional languages of the United States | 9: Arabic, Chinese (Simplified), Chinese (Traditional), French, Korean, Portuguese (Brazil), Russian, Spanish (Mexico) and Vietnamese |

Many ASO guides still name only Spanish (Mexico) for the United States. asobeast follows Apple's table.

Apple's page lists languages; it does not describe search. In practice, App Store search in a storefront reads the title, subtitle and keyword field of every localization on that storefront's list, and the words of one localization combine into phrases only with each other. Each additional language is therefore extra room: its own 30 character title, 30 character subtitle and 100 byte keyword field.

asobeast cannot see which of those localizations your listing fills. App Store Connect is private and a store page shows one localization per request, so the metadata workbench lists what each storefront reads without guessing what you have. See [Optimize listing metadata](/guides/metadata-workbench#see-which-localizations-each-storefront-reads).

Google Play has no such list. It shows the listing that matches the device language, so storefront localizations are App Store only.
