Every hosted store request leaves through the egress pool. When the pool degrades, collection slows for every customer at once, and the worst version of the failure is the quiet one: endpoints that answer with plausible but wrong results.

## Symptoms

- `pool.healthy.low` pages, or `pool.blocked.rising` and `pool.silent.rising` arrive for the same day.
- `asobeast_pool_endpoints_healthy` falls while `asobeast_pool_endpoints` holds steady.
- `asobeast_workspace_rankings_unresolved` climbs across many workspaces at once, which is what a silent failure looks like from the customer side.
- Worker concurrency drops on its own, because it tracks the healthy pool size.
- Google Play jobs for one storefront wait or time out while endpoints in other countries sit idle. That storefront's own endpoints are saturated, because a queued Google Play job never leaves through another country while one of its own is healthy. Each endpoint carries one job at a time, so a storefront with one endpoint finishes roughly one Google Play scoring job a minute and a backlog beyond that waits its turn in the queue. See [Egress proxy pool](/configuration/reference#egress-proxy-pool).
- The API log names a refused tunnel, `refused by the proxy (HTTP 402)`, and **Admin, Capacity** says how many endpoints the proxy provider refused and why while they rest. The provider answered the tunnel itself, so no request reached a store.
- Imports, new competitors or refreshes answer `503` "This server is busy reaching Google Play." while queued work runs. Queued work already leaves `PROXY_INTERACTIVE_RESERVE` endpoints free, so this means the reserve itself is taken: several people wait at once, or the pool is so small that the reserve is ignored. The API log shows `acquire failed: no proxy endpoint was free within the request budget` at warn. Raise the reserve by one, or add endpoints.

## Triage

<Steps>
  <Step title="Read the pool">
    `GET /admin/proxy-pool` reports per store health, per endpoint outcomes and residential spend. The `alerts` array repeats what fired.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Classify the outcome that dominates">
    | Last outcome | Cause | First move |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | `TRANSPORT` | The provider or the network, not the store | Wait one cycle, then open a provider ticket |
    | `RATE_LIMITED` | The pool is being paced too hard | Lower `SCRAPE_ITUNES_RPM` or `SCRAPE_GPLAY_RPM` |
    | `BLOCKED` | A store is refusing the address range | Continue below |
    | `SILENT` | Requests succeed and return results that are not real | Treat as the most serious. Continue below |
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read why the provider refuses">
    A proxy can refuse the tunnel before any request reaches a store. A lease is judged by the refusal when every failure it saw was a refused tunnel, whether a refusal ended the work or the work answered anyway or ended in a failure that says nothing about the network, such as a parse failure. The endpoint then records `TRANSPORT` and keeps the refused status until its next success or its next failure that no refusal judged. `GET /admin/proxy-pool` reports it in `lastRefusalStatus` only while the endpoint is still cooling down, so a rarely used endpoint stops naming an old refusal once the provider lets requests through again, and **Admin, Capacity** explains it from the status alone, whatever the provider:

    | Status | What it means | First move | Endpoint | Queued work |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | `402` | The plan or its quota is used up, often the monthly bandwidth | Top up or upgrade the plan with the provider | Rests a fixed five minutes | Waits five minutes without spending a retry |
    | `407` | The provider does not accept the proxy credentials | Check the proxy username and password the provider issued | Rests a fixed five minutes | Waits five minutes without spending a retry |
    | Any other | The provider refused for its own reason | Open a provider ticket with the status and the endpoint addresses | Backs off from 30 seconds, doubling with each failure in a row, up to one hour | Retries as usual, so it can reach another endpoint |

    A `402` or a `407` refuses every endpoint of the account at once, so a lease that refusal judged is retried after a fixed five minutes without growing the endpoint's failure streak, and collection resumes within minutes of the account being put right. Any other refused status can be one bad address, so it grows the streak and backs off like any network failure, starting at 30 seconds and never resting longer than one hour. A block, a rate limit, an answer that is not real or another network failure in the same lease is judged as before: its own cooldown applies, the streak grows and no refused status is kept. Queued work whose last failure is a `402` or a `407` goes back to the queue for those five minutes, unless a worse outcome (a block, a rate limit or an answer that is not real) was seen in the same lease, as it does when no endpoint is free, and spends none of its retries while the account is down. A refusal the work got past before failing another way, a parse failure included, keeps that failure and its usual retries, and so does any other refused status, which can be one bad address. A request someone waits for still answers that the store could not be reached. A refusal on its own never starts the residential fallback, because the fallback answers a store block, not a provider account; only a block the store itself gave in the same lease does.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Separate one store from both">
    Blocking on one store with the other healthy is a store decision about the range. Both stores failing at once is almost always the provider.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Containment

<Warning>
  A silent failure writes wrong data. Contain it before you fix it, because every hour it runs adds ranking rows a customer will act on.
</Warning>

1. **Silent failures**: pause the affected store's queue from `/admin/queues`. No capture is better than a wrong capture.
2. **Blocking**: leave the pool to cool down. Blocked endpoints are already parked, and forcing them back accelerates the block.
3. **Rate limiting**: reduce the store's requests per minute and let the current run finish late rather than fail.
4. Do not raise the residential cap to ride out a datacenter block. Residential egress is metered and priced per gigabyte, and a mixed customer costs several times their subscription there.

## Recovery

1. Buy more datacenter endpoints from the provider and let `CRON_PROXY_SYNC` add them, or run the sync early.
2. Confirm `asobeast_pool_endpoints_healthy` recovers above half of `asobeast_pool_endpoints` for both stores.
3. If a store blocks the whole range, the options in order of preference are: a different datacenter range, and residential egress for a small share of traffic with `PROXY_RESIDENTIAL_MONTHLY_CAP_USD` set.
4. Delete ranking rows captured during a confirmed silent window. They are worse than a gap.
5. Unpause the queue and watch one full daily cycle.

## Who to tell

- Customers, if collection paused for longer than one daily cycle. Say which store is delayed. A delay that is explained reads as a delay; silence reads as broken data.
- The proxy provider, with endpoint addresses and outcome counts, for anything that looks like a transport fault.
- Nobody outside, for a block. It is a normal cost of the business and does not affect customer data.
