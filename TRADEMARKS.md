## What you can do without asking

- Run AsoBeast for yourself or inside your own organization with the name and logo in place, including on a subdomain of your own such as `asobeast.example.com`.
- Pass on unchanged official releases and images under the name, including a template that installs them on the user's own server or account.
- Say truthfully that you use AsoBeast, or that your project is based on it or is a fork of it.
- Name an add-on, integration or tool "Something for AsoBeast", never "AsoBeast Something", with your own name as the prominent one.
- Write and talk about AsoBeast, compare it with other tools, and show unaltered screenshots and the unaltered logo when you do.
- Run a meetup or user group, or translate the documentation, as long as it says it is unofficial and community run.
- Keep internal identifiers that carry the name, such as the `@asobeast/*` package scope, environment variables and database names.

## What needs permission first

- Offering a service under the AsoBeast name or logo to people outside your organization, free or paid, even when the code is unchanged. Managed hosting, resale, hosting for clients and a public demo all count.
- Publishing a changed build under the name. The names of public packages, container images and registry entries count.
- Using the name or the logo, or anything confusingly similar such as "AsoBest", "ASO Beast" or "AsoBeast Cloud", in the name of a product, company, domain, app or extension listing, or in a social media account that passes as official.
- Using the name or logo in adverts for your own product or service, beyond a truthful comparison, or on merchandise.
- Suggesting that your service or fork is official, endorsed by AsoBeast or affiliated with it.
- Altering the logo, or using it as part of your own branding.
- Registering the name or the logo, or anything confusingly similar, as a trademark or a domain.

## Forks

A fork that other people can use or download needs its own name and logo wherever its users see them, including the interface and emails. It may say truthfully that it is a fork of AsoBeast or based on it.
