# Review rules

### Domain rule 21: A review reply is a stored row the owner approves; the store is written only by a person's click

`ReviewReply` (tenant owned, RLS, `@@id([reviewId])`, cascades with the review and the app) holds one reply per review: its `text`, how it was made (`tone` validated in code against `REVIEW_REPLY_TONES`, `model`, `generatedAt`, all null for a hand written reply) and the owner's `repliedAt` mark. "Replied" has two sources and `replyStateOf` in `@asobeast/shared` is the one rule for a loaded row, `replyStateWhere` (`reviews/review-list-query.ts`) the same rule as a query: `Review.repliedAt`, which the daily Google Play sync records with the reply's text in `Review.replyText`, or `ReviewReply.repliedAt`; a row without a mark is `drafted`; the App Store feed carries no reply, so an App Store review is replied only by the mark. `ReviewRepliesService` writes the row by hand and `App.replyGuidelines`, holding the trimmed text to `REVIEW_REPLY_LIMITS[store]` counted with `countChars` (`400` past it; the DTO `MaxLength` is only the larger bound), refuses a body with neither text nor a non-null `replied` with `400` before any lookup (a `null` mark is no mark and never creates an empty row; a body with text is unaffected), and deletes the row when a mark is undone with no text left; `ReviewReplyAssistant` drafts through one `AiGateway.spend` under `reviewReply`, from the review, the app, the newest home snapshot's version and release notes, the guidelines, up to three recent Play replies, the tone and the owner's instruction line, never another review or another app, fits the answer to the store limit on a sentence boundary (`fitReply`, never inside a surrogate pair) and reports `truncated` instead of spending a second call, and never touches `repliedAt`. The review, the release notes and the previous replies are untrusted in the prompt and travel as one JSON block closed by `END_OF_REFERENCE`, so a label typed into a review cannot read as an owner line; the guidelines and the instruction line sit above it, are the owner's and may steer. A draft that holds a link or an email address neither of them contains is refused with `502` (`foreignContacts`), and the call stays counted because the model answered. `ReviewReply` references its review through `(reviewId, appId)`, so a reply can only name a review of its own app. Google Play publishes a 350 character limit and Apple publishes none, so `APP_STORE: 2000` is asobeast's own ceiling. The composer on the reviews page works with no key; AI fills the textarea and nothing else. It measures the text with `measureReply` (`components/reviews/reply-text.ts`), the trimmed text the service counts, so the counter, `aria-invalid`, **Copy** and **Mark as replied** read one measurement. It never discards on blur: empty text cannot be stored, a discard also removes the owner's mark and an AI draft's provenance, and blur fires while the owner is still editing, so an emptied field shows a hint and **Discard** behind its confirmation stays the one way to remove a reply. Google's reply guidelines forbid soliciting, so no draft asks for a better rating, promotes, or adds a link or an address the guidelines do not contain. `GET /apps/{id}/reviews` filters by `replyState` and by `from`, the first UTC day a review was written, sorts `newest` or `lowest`, embeds `reply` on every row and answers `counts` under the star, version and `from` filters, never under `replyState`. A fourth state, `updated`, is a Google Play review whose `reviewedAt` is after the store's `repliedAt`: the daily sync takes a newer edit of a review it already stored (`storedReviewChanges`, score, title, text, version and date, no alert) and a later reply date, and `replyStateWhere` compares the two columns through the client's field reference, so it takes `prisma.review.fields.repliedAt` rather than building one. The owner's mark never makes a review `updated`, and an App Store review never is. `GET /apps/{id}/reviews/themes` answers phrases that stand out (`reviewThemes`, built on `reviewPhrases`, the extraction the keyword ideas use, here with up to four words and `themeIgnoredWords` kept off a phrase's edges): each phrase of the newest `REVIEW_THEMES_BASELINE` reviews under the version and `from` filters gets a binomial surprise score for its share of reviews at or below `ALERT_REVIEW_SCORE_MAX` against the period's smoothed share; the side most reviews fall on is judged by lean (a phrase at least as low, or as high, rated as the period) and the other side by `REVIEW_THEME_SURPRISE`, each with at least `REVIEW_THEME_MIN_REVIEWS`; the counts answered are those of the first `REVIEW_THEMES_CAP` (200, the most a search loads) reviews under every list filter in the list's `sort`, a phrase needs `REVIEW_THEME_MIN_REVIEWS` of them, and only then is a shorter phrase dropped for a longer one covering three quarters of its mentions, so a star filter narrows the counts and never the judgement. Each review drops the function words of the storefront's languages and of its own guessed language, or, when the guess is null, of every language that ties for the most function words it holds (`reviewLanguages`), so a short review no guess can place is still read in the languages it could be. It calls no store and no model. A review translation lives on the `Review` row (`translationLanguage`, `sourceLanguage`, `translatedTitle`, `translatedText`, `translatedAt`), one language at a time: `ReviewTranslator` answers the kept translation for the same language without spending, otherwise spends one `AiGateway` call under `reviewTranslation` after every check that can refuse (an unknown review, no words, an unknown language), and the sync clears it (`TRANSLATION_CLEARED`) when an edit changes the title or the text; the write is matched on the title and text that were translated, so a translation of text the sync just replaced is answered and never kept. The review travels in the same untrusted block as a draft, and an answer holding a link or an address the review does not is refused with `502` (`foreignContacts`). `ReviewItem.language` is a read layer guess (`reviewLanguage`: script first, then function words, `null` when unsure) that only decides where the web offers **Translate**, falling back to the storefront's language. `reviews.reply_negative` keeps reading the store's reply on Google Play only; the owner's mark is not an input to the rule, and its link opens `?replyState=needs_reply&sort=lowest`. `POST` and `DELETE /apps/{id}/reviews/{reviewId}/reply/post` (`ReviewReplyPoster`) send the kept `ReviewReply.text` and take no body; both are `@SessionOnly()`, open to members, synchronous, never queued or retried. A post holds the text to the store limit before any request, opens the store's connection (`openAppStoreReviews`, `openPlayPublisher`, `409` without a usable one), and on success writes both halves of the reply state in one transaction: `ReviewReply.postedAt`, `postedText`, `postState` (`pending` until Apple publishes, `published` for Google, validated against `REVIEW_POST_STATES`), `storeResponseId`, `postedByUserId` and `repliedAt`, and `Review.repliedAt` and `replyText`, so `replyStateOf`, `replyStateWhere`, the counts and `reviews.reply_negative` read a real reply with no new state. The App Store feed's review id is not App Store Connect's, so `AscReviewLocator` lists the home territory's customer reviews newest first down to three days before `reviewedAt` (at most `ASC_REVIEW_LOCATE_MAX_PAGES` without a date), `matchCustomerReview` matches rating, title, body and nickname and refuses two candidates it cannot tell apart, and the found id is kept in `ReviewReply.storeReviewId` for every later post and the withdraw (a kept id that answers `404` is located again once). Apple overwrites a response on a second `POST` and Google edits in place, so an update is a second post; `isPostEdited` derives "edited since posting". A withdraw exists for the App Store only (`DELETE /v1/customerReviewResponses/{id}`), clears the post columns and the two `Review` columns and keeps the text and `storeReviewId`; on Google Play it answers `409`. A `403` on a review resource is a missing role, never a rejected key: it sets `StoreConnection.replyAccess` to `forbidden` (`allowed` after a success, reset by a verify or a new key) and answers `403`, and the connection stays active. A Google sign in posts through its refresh token only when it granted the `androidpublisher` scope (`GoogleAccountCredentials.canReply`); one that did not is `forbidden` from connect and answers `409` with `PLAY_REPLIES_NEED_CONSENT`. The store errors map to HTTP in `review-reply-post-errors.ts`. A review spike is judged by the store's `reviewedAt` day and never by the day a review was stored, so a backfill or a resumed app cannot raise one.
